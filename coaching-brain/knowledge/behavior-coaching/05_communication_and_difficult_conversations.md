# Communication & Difficult Conversations

> Sources: Motivational Interviewing communication principles (Miller & Rollnick — reflective listening, autonomy support, rolling with discord); SDT (autonomy/competence/relatedness in coach communication); feedback and behavior-change communication practice; coaching scope-of-practice and refer-out standards. ## 0. How the copilot uses this file

Communication is where retention, adherence, and trust are won or lost. This file covers *how the coach talks* — everyday principles, feedback, and the hard conversations most coaches avoid (money, boundaries, referrals) until they cost a client or a reputation. The copilot should use it to (a) draft what the coach says, and (b) flag when a conversation is heading somewhere that needs a boundary or a referral.

---

## 1. Core communication principles

1. **Listen more than you talk.** MI's central finding applies everywhere: clients change and stay when they feel *heard*. Reflective listening ("it sounds like…") beats advice-giving for building trust and surfacing the real issue.
2. **Autonomy-supportive tone (SDT).** Offer choices, give rationale ("here's *why* we're doing this"), and respect that it's her decision. Controlling language ("you have to," "you must," "you should") breeds resistance. Swap "you need to" → "what would work for you is…" or "one option is…".
3. **Warmth + relatedness.** Personal, human, specific. Remember life details, use voice notes over templates, celebrate real wins. Premium coaching is a relationship, not a content feed.
4. **Clarity and honesty.** Say the real thing kindly. Vague, over-softened communication leaves clients confused and expectations unmet (a top churn cause).
5. **Positive-leaning ratio.** Affirm specifics before you correct. Clients who feel like they're constantly failing quit. (See feedback, §2.)
6. **Match the channel to the message.** Wins and quick nudges → text/app. Sensitive or emotional topics (a hard plateau talk, a boundary, a referral) → voice note or call, never a cold text. Bad news over text reads worse than intended.
7. **Responsiveness within agreed limits.** Fast, caring responses build trust — but within boundaries you set (§4), or you burn out and train clients to expect 24/7 access.

> **Copilot default voice:** warm, specific, honest, choice-offering, and light on correction. When drafting a coach message, lead with a genuine affirmation, state the point clearly, offer an option rather than a command, and end with a question or a small next step.

## 2. Giving feedback (so it lands and changes behavior)

Feedback fails when it's all criticism, vague, or ego-bruising (kills competence, SDT). Use a clear, kind structure:

**The feedback pattern (coach it to the coach):**
1. **Affirm something specific and true first.** "You hit all three sessions this week — that consistency is exactly what's driving your strength up." (Not empty praise; real and specific.)
2. **State the adjustment plainly, without a moral charge.** "The area to tighten is protein — you're averaging about half your target." (Behavior, not character. "The protein's low," not "you're not trying.")
3. **Give the why + a concrete next step.** "Protein protects muscle while we're in a deficit, so let's anchor it to breakfast — 'after coffee, I make eggs.' Sound doable?" (Rationale = autonomy; specific step = ability.)
4. **Check in / invite her voice.** "How does that land? Anything getting in the way?" (Collaboration, not decree.)

**Do:**
- Be specific ("your squat depth improved" beats "good job").
- Focus on process and effort she controls, not just outcomes.
- Normalize difficulty ("this part is hard for almost everyone").

**Avoid:**
- The "compliment sandwich" done clumsily (fake praise around a criticism — clients see through it). Real affirmation + clear point + supportive next step beats a formulaic sandwich.
- Piling multiple corrections at once (overwhelm → paralysis). One key adjustment per check-in.
- Body-shaming or negative body commentary of any kind (file 04 §8).

## 3. Hard conversation: missed / late payments

Uncomfortable but essential — avoiding it costs money and breeds resentment. Keep it **professional, warm, and matter-of-fact**; separate the person from the transaction.

**Principles:**
- Have clear payment terms *up front* (in the onboarding agreement) so this is enforcing an agreement, not making a new demand.
- Address it promptly and privately, not passive-aggressively or publicly.
- Assume good faith first (cards expire, people forget) — open non-accusatorially.
- Keep the relationship intact: the coaching relationship and the payment issue are separate threads.

**Script (first, gentle nudge):**
> "Hi [name] — quick admin note, looks like this month's payment didn't come through. No rush, probably just a card thing. Could you sort it when you get a sec? Thanks so much!"

**Script (follow-up, still warm but firmer):**
> "Hi [name], just following up on the outstanding payment for this cycle. I want to keep everything running smoothly for you — can we get this settled by [date]? Happy to jump on a quick call if anything's come up on your end."

**If it's a genuine hardship:** it's okay to offer a one-off pause or a plan — on *your* terms, clearly bounded, not as an open-ended freebie. Protect your business and your boundaries.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Draft a warm, brief, assume-good-faith nudge referencing the agreed terms. Keep the coaching relationship separate from the money. If unpaid after the nudge, follow up with a clear date and an offer to talk. Don't let it fester or go public."

## 4. Hard conversation: boundary-setting

Premium, high-touch coaching invites over-reach — messaging at all hours, expecting instant replies, wanting more than the package. Boundaries protect the coach's energy *and* the client's respect. Set them early and reinforce kindly.

**Principles:**
- **Set expectations at onboarding:** response times, contactable hours, what the package includes vs. doesn't, and the communication channel. A boundary stated up front isn't a rejection later.
- **Reinforce warmly, not defensively.** You can hold a line and be kind.
- **Autonomy cuts both ways** — you get to run your business on your terms.

**Scripts:**
- *Setting response-time expectation (onboarding):* "So you always know what to expect: I check messages and reply within 24 hours on weekdays. For anything urgent health-wise, please contact a doctor — I'm your coach, not your medical line."
- *Reinforcing when tested:* "I saw your messages last night — I keep evenings offline so I'm fresh for you during the day. I've got you first thing tomorrow."
- *Scope creep (wants more than the package):* "Great question — that's actually beyond what this program covers, but it's exactly what the [next tier / add-on] is built for. Want me to walk you through it?"

> **Copilot response pattern:** "If a client is testing boundaries (off-hours messaging, scope creep, expecting 24/7 access), draft a kind, clear restatement of the agreed terms — warmth + firmness, no apology or defensiveness. If it's recurring, recommend the coach revisit expectations explicitly. Boundaries protect retention, they don't threaten it."

## 5. Hard conversation: when to refer out (the scope-of-practice line)

**This is the most important guardrail in the whole brain.** A fitness coach is not a doctor, physiotherapist, registered dietitian, or mental-health professional. Operating outside scope risks the client's safety, the coach's liability, and LFG's reputation. The skill is *recognizing the line early* and referring *warmly* so it strengthens trust rather than feeling like abandonment.

**Refer out when you see:**

| Domain | Red flags → refer to | 
|---|---|
| **Injury / pain** | Pain (not soreness), joint pain, swelling, numbness, post-surgery, suspected injury → **physiotherapist / sports doctor** |
| **Illness / medical** | Chest pain, breathlessness, dizziness/fainting, uncontrolled BP, new/undiagnosed symptoms, chronic disease management, pregnancy complications, medication questions → **GP / physician** |
| **Clinical nutrition** | Managing a medical condition through diet (diabetes, IBD, kidney disease, clinical eating plans), pregnancy nutrition beyond general guidance → **registered dietitian / doctor** |
| **Disordered eating** | Extreme restriction, purging, compulsive over-exercise, obsessive food rules, secrecy, dangerous weight loss, intense body distress → **eating-disorder-specialist dietitian / clinical psychologist** (file 04 §8) |
| **Mental health** | Signs of depression, severe anxiety, trauma, self-harm/suicidal ideation → **licensed mental-health professional / GP** (and for crisis, direct to emergency/crisis services immediately) |

**How to refer without losing the relationship (warm hand-off):**
> "This is a bit outside what I'm qualified to help with, and I care too much about your health to guess. I'd really like you to see a [physio / doctor / dietitian] for this — I can keep supporting you on everything within my lane in the meantime, and we'll work together with what they advise."

**Principles:** refer *early* (don't wait until it's serious), frame it as *care and standards* (not "not my problem"), stay in your lane while they get specialist help, and keep the client engaged on what you *can* coach.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Scope flag first: if the situation touches injury, illness, clinical nutrition, disordered eating, or mental health, recommend a warm refer-out to the right professional — never coach through it. Draft the hand-off as care + standards, and suggest what the coach can still support in-scope so the client stays engaged. For any crisis (self-harm/suicidal ideation, chest pain, fainting), advise directing to emergency/crisis services immediately."

## 6. Cultural sensitivity — Dubai / GCC, largely-female clientele

LFG's clients are mostly female professionals in a GCC context. Communication that ignores this reads as tone-deaf and costs trust. Layer these into everything (cross-references file 04 §10):

- **Privacy & modesty as default, not exception.** Don't request progress photos; offer measurements/performance instead. Get explicit consent before any public shout-out or sharing a win, and always allow opt-out. Some clients want zero visibility — respect it fully.
- **Female-only comfort.** Female-only spaces, discretion, and a coach presence that respects modesty lower the barrier to entry and keep clients comfortable. Be mindful of how body-focused language and imagery land.
- **Religious observance (Ramadan & fasting).** Proactively adjust programming, expectations, and messaging around Ramadan and other observances. Don't schedule intense demands during fasting hours; acknowledge and *plan for* the month rather than treating the dip as a failure. Respect prayer times in scheduling where relevant.
- **Halal & food culture.** Nutrition talk must fit halal eating and the social role of food (family meals, hospitality). Coach around her real culture, not against it — never frame social/family eating as "cheating."
- **Body-image sensitivity in a cultural frame.** Aesthetic pressure and body talk can be especially fraught; lead with health, strength, energy, and capability over appearance.
- **Tone & respect.** Professional, warm, respectful of family and cultural commitments (which will legitimately take priority over training at times — meet it with flexibility, not guilt).
- **Language & directness.** Calibrate directness to the individual; some clients prefer gentle indirect feedback. Read the person, stay respectful, avoid slang or humor that may not translate.

> **Copilot cross-cutting rule for all client communication:** run every message and plan through three cultural filters — *privacy/modesty, religious observance (Ramadan/fasting/prayer), and food culture (halal/social eating)* — and adjust tone and content before it goes out.
