# Client Situations Playbook

> Sources: synthesizes files 01–03 (Fogg B=MAP, Atomic Habits, SDT, Stages of Change, MI, lapse/adherence research, retention data) into situational decision rules. Body-image and disordered-eating guidance follows a hard scope-of-practice line (refer out to licensed professionals). Original explanations, no verbatim text.

---

## 0. How the copilot uses this file

This is the **"what's the best next move?"** engine. Each situation has the same structure so the copilot can respond fast and consistently:

- **Signals** — how to recognize it.
- **Diagnose** — the questions/framework to find the real cause (usually: which B=MAP lever, which SDT need, which stage of change).
- **Best next move** — the recommended action.
- **Avoid** — the common mistake that makes it worse.
- **Copilot response pattern** — a compact template for how the AI phrases advice back to the coach in the moment.

**Golden rules across every situation:**
1. Diagnose before prescribing. "Unmotivated" and "lazy" are never diagnoses.
2. Fix the cheapest lever first: **Prompt → Ability → Motivation** (file 01).
3. Never shame. Shame drives clients out.
4. Stay in scope. Injury, illness, disordered eating, clinical mental health, and clinical nutrition → **refer out** (see the hard line in §4, §8, and file 05).

## 1. The unmotivated client

**Signals:** low energy in messages, skipping sessions, "I just can't get myself to do it," going quiet.

**Diagnose:** "Unmotivated" is a symptom, not a cause. Run the B=MAP triage (file 01 §1):
- Missing **prompt**? (Nothing cued the workout.)
- Too hard / no **time** (ability)? (The real #1 stated dropout reason.)
- Genuinely doesn't want it (motivation)? → which **SDT need** is starved — autonomy (feels bossed around), competence (no visible progress), or relatedness (feels alone/like a number)?
- What **stage of change** is she really in? Ambivalent clients get pushed too hard.

**Best next move:** Don't try to inject motivation. (a) Shrink the behavior to a can't-fail size (10-min session, one habit). (b) Add/strengthen the prompt (habit stack, calendar block, WhatsApp nudge). (c) Use MI to reconnect her *own* "why" (file 02 evoking questions + scaling). (d) If competence is the gap, surface a non-scale win. (e) If relatedness is the gap, get her to run club/bootcamp and add a personal touch.

**Avoid:** Lecturing, "you just have to want it more," piling on guilt, or making the plan bigger.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Before motivating her, diagnose: has a prompt been firing, and is the session small enough to do on a bad day? Recommend shrinking to a 10-min minimum + one habit stack, then an MI check-in ('on 0–10 how important is this — why not lower?') to surface her own reasons. Flag which SDT need looks starved."

## 2. The plateau (progress stalls)

**Signals:** scale/measurements flat for 2–3+ weeks, frustration, "nothing's working," motivation dipping.

**Diagnose:** Separate a *real* plateau from *normal variation and mis-measurement* (file 07):
- Is it actually a plateau, or a 1–2 week weight fluctuation (water, hormones, sodium, Ramadan feeding shifts, cycle phase)? A trend needs 2–3+ weeks.
- Is adherence actually where she thinks? (Honest audit of intake/steps/sessions — plateaus are often drifted adherence, not metabolic.)
- Has the body adapted (need progressive overload / a training or nutrition variable change)?
- Is the *only* metric the scale? (Body recomp: weight flat while measurements and performance improve = progress, not a plateau.)

**Best next move:** (a) Reframe with data — show non-scale progress (performance PRs, measurements, photos-or-alternatives) so competence isn't destroyed. (b) Audit adherence honestly and non-judgmentally. (c) Change ONE variable (calories, steps, training stimulus, or a diet break/refeed) — never five at once. (d) Reset expectations: plateaus are a normal, expected phase, not failure.

**Avoid:** Slashing calories hard + adding cardio + changing the whole program at once (unsustainable, and you learn nothing). Panicking with her.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "First confirm it's a real plateau (2–3 wk trend, not a fluctuation) and check adherence honestly. Then show her the non-scale wins to protect morale, and change ONE variable. Recommend which single lever (calories / steps / training stimulus / diet break) fits her data. Reassure that plateaus are expected."

## 3. Poor adherence (not doing the work despite saying she will)

**Signals:** intention–action gap — agrees to the plan, then doesn't execute; missed logs; "I know what to do, I just don't do it."

**Diagnose:** Classic B=MAP failure — almost always **prompt or ability**, rarely willpower. Also check: is the plan too big/complex (PN: one habit at a time)? Was the commitment set at high confidence (≥9/10)? Is there a life-stress driver (§6)?

**Best next move:** (a) Shrink to a single habit at ≥9/10 confidence (if she's not that confident, it's too big). (b) Install implementation intentions + habit stacking ("after coffee, fill water bottle"; "Mon/Wed/Fri 6:30am at the DIFC gym"). (c) Add self-monitoring — the strongest evidence-backed adherence lever (a simple daily log/tick). (d) Design the environment (pack the bag, remove friction). (e) Add a satisfying reward + accountability check-in.

**Avoid:** Adding *more* to the plan, or interpreting non-adherence as a character flaw. The plan failed her, not the reverse.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "This is an ability/prompt problem, not discipline. Recommend: cut the plan to ONE habit she's ≥9/10 confident she'll do, attach it to an existing routine (habit stack) with a specific when/where, and add a 1-tap daily log. Remove one friction point. Do not add volume."

## 4. Injury / illness

**Signals:** reports pain, an injury, sickness, a flare-up, post-surgery, or a new medical diagnosis.

**⚠️ HARD SCOPE LINE:** A coach is not a physio, doctor, or physician. **Do NOT diagnose, treat, rehab a suspected injury, or "train through" pain.** This is the clearest refer-out in coaching.

**Diagnose (scope-appropriate only):** Is it soreness (normal DOMS) or pain/injury (stop)? Acute vs. ongoing? Any red flags (sharp/joint pain, swelling, numbness, chest pain/breathlessness, illness beyond mild)?

**Best next move:** (a) **Refer to the appropriate professional** — physiotherapist, sports doctor, or GP — for anything beyond ordinary soreness. Say so plainly. (b) Pause/modify: never load through pain. (c) Keep her *engaged* while she heals — train around it *only within cleared limits* (e.g. upper-body work with a lower-limb issue), protect nutrition habits, keep the relationship and community warm so she doesn't churn during downtime (a top silent-dropout window). (d) Return-to-training only when cleared, and rebuild gradually.

**Avoid:** Playing doctor, prescribing rehab exercises for a specific injury, pushing "no pain no gain," or going silent while she's out (she'll drift and quit).

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Scope flag: refer to a physio/doctor for anything past normal soreness — do not diagnose or rehab it. Advise pausing the aggravating movement, training only around it within cleared limits, and keeping her engaged (community + nutrition habits) so she doesn't churn during recovery. Plan a gradual return once cleared."

## 5. Unrealistic expectations

**Signals:** wants dramatic results on a crash timeline ("10kg in a month for the wedding"), compares to filtered social media, disappointed by healthy-pace progress.

**Diagnose:** Where did the expectation come from (social media, a past crash diet, an event deadline)? Is it a timeline problem, a magnitude problem, or a method problem (wanting extreme methods)? This is usually an **onboarding gap** surfacing late (file 03/06).

**Best next move:** (a) Educate honestly and kindly — realistic rates of fat loss / strength / running progress, and *why* slower is more durable (crash approaches rebound). (b) Reframe around process goals she controls and the non-scale wins she'll feel first (file 01 §7, file 07). (c) Anchor to a real, motivating event goal if there is one (5k, HYROX) — channel the urgency into something healthy. (d) Use MI, not confrontation — don't crush the dream, redirect it: "Let's aim for the strongest, leanest version of you by the wedding, sustainably — here's what's genuinely possible."

**Avoid:** Agreeing to unsafe crash targets to keep her happy, OR bluntly deflating her (kills motivation). Also avoid promising outcomes you can't control.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Reset expectations without killing motivation. Give her the honest realistic rate + why sustainable beats crash, then redirect the urgency into process goals and an event anchor. Use MI framing ('here's what's genuinely achievable by [date]'). Flag this as an onboarding-expectations issue to prevent next time."

## 6. Life stress / overwhelm (work, family, burnout)

**Signals:** "everything's crazy right now," missed sessions clustered around a work/family event, exhaustion, wanting to pause or quit.

**Diagnose:** Is this a temporary spike (deadline, travel, kids' exams, Ramadan, moving) or a chronic overload? Ability lever is crushed; motivation follows. Is she catastrophizing one bad week into "I've failed"? (Abstinence-violation effect — file 01 §8.)

**Best next move:** (a) **Meet her where she is** — switch to a "maintenance mode" mini-plan (2 short sessions, protect sleep + protein + steps) rather than all-or-nothing. Doing *something* keeps the habit and identity alive. (b) Reduce, don't cancel — the goal is to not miss twice. (c) Empathize genuinely (relatedness) and remove pressure (autonomy) — MI rolling-with-resistance. (d) Reframe: fitness is a stress *reliever*, not another chore; a short walk/run is self-care. (e) Keep her connected to community, gently.

**Avoid:** Guilt-tripping, demanding "make-up" work, or letting her go fully dark (silent-dropout risk). Also avoid ignoring genuine burnout — if it's clinical-level stress/anxiety, that's a refer-out (file 05).

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Shift her to a scaled 'maintenance mode' (2 short sessions + protect sleep/protein/steps), not all-or-nothing. Lead with empathy, remove pressure, and frame training as stress relief. Keep her lightly connected to the community. Watch for clinical-level burnout → refer out if so."

## 7. Comparison / impatience (social-media effect)

**Signals:** "why isn't it happening faster," comparing to influencers or other clients, discouraged despite real progress.

**Diagnose:** Competence and autonomy under threat via external comparison. Is she measuring against filtered/edited content or people on different timelines/genetics/PEDs? Is impatience masking an unrealistic expectation (§5) or invisible-progress problem (file 07)?

**Best next move:** (a) Redirect comparison inward — her only fair benchmark is her own week-0 self; show the tracked progress. (b) Curate her environment (Atomic Habits): suggest unfollowing accounts that spike comparison, following ones that inspire realistically. (c) Reinforce process goals and the compounding nature of consistency ("this is a season, not a sprint"). (d) Celebrate her specific wins loudly (competence).

**Avoid:** Dismissing her feelings ("don't compare" alone doesn't work) or feeding the comparison by benchmarking her against other clients.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Redirect the comparison to her own baseline with tracked data, and coach environment design (unfollow comparison triggers). Reinforce that consistency compounds over a season. Celebrate her specific wins to rebuild competence. Don't just say 'stop comparing.'"

## 8. Body-image sensitivity

**Signals:** harsh self-talk ("I hate my body," "I'm disgusting"), distress at photos/measurements/scale, avoidance of tracking, extreme fixation on weight, all-or-nothing food language.

**⚠️ HARD SCOPE LINE:** Watch for disordered-eating / body-dysmorphia red flags — extreme restriction, purging, compulsive over-exercise, obsessive food rules, secrecy around eating, weight loss becoming dangerous, intense distress. **If these appear, do NOT coach through them.** Refer to a licensed professional (registered dietitian specializing in eating disorders / clinical psychologist / GP). A coach's role is early recognition and warm referral, not treatment (file 05).

**Diagnose (within scope):** Is this normal insecurity/dissatisfaction, or clinical-level? How does she talk about her body and food? Is the scale/photo process itself causing harm?

**Best next move (normal-range only):** (a) **Offer non-scale, non-photo tracking** — measurements, performance, habits, energy, how clothes fit (file 07). For photo-averse or modesty-sensitive clients, drop photos entirely; performance and measurements tell the story. (b) Shift language from aesthetics to *capability and health* — "look how strong you're getting" over "look how much smaller you are." (c) Reduce weigh-in frequency or stop the scale if it's harmful. (d) Model compassionate language; never comment on her body negatively. (e) Reinforce that fitness is about strength, energy, health, and how she feels.

**Avoid:** Aesthetic-only framing, before/after pressure, forced photos, "just love yourself" platitudes, and — critically — **coaching a suspected eating disorder yourself.**

> **Copilot response pattern:** "First screen for disordered-eating red flags — if present, warm refer-out to a specialist, don't coach through it (scope line). If normal-range: drop the scale/photos if they're harmful, switch to performance + measurements + how-she-feels tracking (respect modesty), and reframe from aesthetics to strength/health/capability. Never comment negatively on her body."

## 9. The difficult / complaining client

**Signals:** frequent complaints, blaming the program, demanding, boundary-pushing (messaging at all hours, wanting more than the package), skeptical/argumentative, or a mismatch of expectations.

**Diagnose:** Separate types — (a) *unmet-need complainer* (real gap: results, communication, feeling heard); (b) *anxious/scared* client whose fear reads as difficulty; (c) *boundary-tester* (wants more than paid for); (d) *genuine mismatch* (not the right fit). MI lens: much "difficulty" is discord — she feels unheard or pressured (file 02 §4).

**Best next move:** (a) **Listen first, reflect, don't defend** — most complaints deflate when the person feels genuinely heard (MI reflective listening). (b) Address the legitimate core if there is one (fix the real gap). (c) For boundary-pushing, set kind, clear boundaries about scope, response times, and what the package includes (file 05). (d) Stay professional and non-defensive; don't match energy. (e) If it's a true values/fit mismatch or the relationship is toxic, it's okay to have an honest conversation about whether this is the right program — retaining the wrong client at the cost of your standards isn't a win.

**Avoid:** Getting defensive, arguing to "win," people-pleasing past your boundaries, or ignoring a legitimate complaint until she churns and leaves a bad review.

> **Copilot response pattern:** "Diagnose the complaint type first. Lead with reflective listening ('it sounds like you feel X') before any defense — most complaints ease when she feels heard. Fix any legitimate gap; for boundary-pushing, restate scope/response-times kindly and clearly (file 05). If it's a genuine fit mismatch, advise an honest fit conversation rather than clinging on."

## 10. GCC / cultural situations (LFG-specific)

Cross-cutting adjustments the copilot should layer onto any situation above:

- **Ramadan:** expect adherence patterns to change for the month — retrain around post-iftar or pre-suhoor windows, reduce volume/intensity, protect hydration in the eating window, adjust expectations (maintenance often the smart goal). Don't read the normal Ramadan dip as dropout; *plan* for it and it becomes a retention moment, not a churn one. **Fasting + intense training or aggressive deficits can carry health risk — flag medical caution for anyone with conditions and refer to a doctor where relevant.**
- **Modesty & progress photos:** default to offering measurements/performance instead of photos; never pressure. Female-only spaces and privacy in any shared wins (consent + opt-out always).
- **Female-only / privacy sensitivity:** discretion in community shout-outs; private channels; respect that some clients want zero visibility.
- **Halal & food culture:** nutrition guidance must fit halal eating and local/social food contexts (family meals, hospitality culture) — work with her real life, not against it.
- **Hot climate:** hydration as a core habit; outdoor sessions timed to avoid peak heat; heat is a genuine ability barrier May–September to design around, not push through.

> **Copilot cross-cutting rule:** For any GCC client, check three filters before finalizing advice — *Ramadan/fasting timing, modesty/privacy, and heat/hydration* — and adjust the plan and expectations accordingly.
