# Assessment & Intake

> Sources: PAR-Q+ / physical-activity readiness screening standards; ACSM-style pre-participation risk stratification principles; SDT & MI for goal clarification (files 01–02); Precision Nutrition intake/lifestyle-audit practice; movement-screen and fitness-baseline conventions. GCC/female/hybrid context per LFG brief. Original explanations, no verbatim text. **Hard scope line: intake screens for readiness and red flags to decide who to train vs. refer; it is not a medical exam or diagnosis.**

---

## 0. How the copilot uses this file

Intake sets up everything downstream: safety, the plan, the goals, the tracking, and (critically) *expectations* — the mismatch that later drives churn is usually a gap that opened here. The copilot uses this file to (a) make sure the coach screens for safety before prescribing anything, (b) capture the right baseline data to measure progress against, and (c) surface red flags that require a referral *before* training starts.

**The intake has five jobs, in order:**
1. **Health screening / readiness** — is it safe to train, or does she need medical clearance first?
2. **Goal clarification** — what does she actually want, and why?
3. **Lifestyle audit** — what's her real life (so the plan fits it)?
4. **Baselines** — where is she starting (so progress is measurable)?
5. **Expectation-setting** — align on realistic outcomes and how progress will be tracked.

---

## 1. Health screening & readiness (do this FIRST, before any programming)

**⚠️ Non-negotiable:** screen every client for readiness *before* prescribing exercise. This is a safety gate, not paperwork. Use a validated self-screen (PAR-Q+ style) plus a health-history intake.

### PAR-Q+ style readiness questions (if any "yes," proceed cautiously / seek medical clearance)
- Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure?
- Do you feel chest pain/discomfort at rest or during activity?
- Do you ever lose balance from dizziness or lose consciousness?
- Do you have a bone, joint, or soft-tissue problem that could worsen with activity?
- Are you currently pregnant, or recently postpartum? (see §1a)
- Are you on medication for a heart condition or blood pressure?
- Is there any other reason you should not do physical activity?

A "yes" doesn't automatically disqualify — it means **get medical clearance / refer before loading** (file 05). The follow-up PAR-Q+ pathway exists for exactly this.

### Health-history intake to collect
- Current/past medical conditions, surgeries, injuries (and current status).
- Medications & supplements (note only — do not advise on meds; refer for medication questions).
- Family history relevant to cardiac/metabolic risk.
- Pregnancy / postpartum / menstrual-cycle context (with sensitivity and consent).
- Sleep, stress, and energy baseline.
- Prior exercise history and any bad experiences.

### Risk stratification (scope-appropriate)
Broadly sort into: **(a) apparently healthy & cleared** → proceed; **(b) has risk factors/symptoms/conditions** → get medical clearance first; **(c) any red flags (below)** → refer *before* training. When in doubt, refer. A premium brand is protected by caution, not bravado.

### 1a. Special populations → medical clearance, not coach-guessing
- **Pregnancy / postpartum:** requires doctor clearance and appropriately qualified guidance (pre/postnatal). Do not prescribe generic loading. Refer for clearance; coach only within cleared, qualified scope.
- **Chronic conditions** (cardiac, diabetes, hypertension, etc.): medical clearance + often specialist input.
- **Older or long-sedentary clients / high BMI with symptoms:** more conservative start, lower dropout risk if eased in (adherence research: overwhelmed beginners quit).

## 2. Red flags requiring medical referral (the refer-out gate)

Refer **before** or **instead of** training when you see (cross-ref file 05):
- Chest pain/pressure, breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness/fainting.
- Uncontrolled or unknown blood pressure; known cardiac disease without clearance.
- Undiagnosed pain, or an unresolved injury.
- Pregnancy/postpartum without clearance; pregnancy complications.
- Signs of disordered eating (extreme restriction, purging, compulsive exercise, obsessive food rules, dangerous weight loss) — refer to a specialist, do not build a fat-loss plan (file 04 §8).
- Signs of clinical mental-health issues (severe depression/anxiety, self-harm ideation) → mental-health referral; crisis → emergency services.
- Any medical condition requiring clinical nutrition management → registered dietitian.

> **Copilot rule:** At intake, run the readiness screen first. If any red flag appears, the next move is a referral and clearance — NOT a program. Flag it explicitly to the coach and draft the warm hand-off (file 05 §5).

## 3. Goal clarification (MI + SDT, not just "what's your goal?")

Surface-level goals ("lose weight," "tone up") are weak foundations. Dig for the *real* motivation and translate it into measurable, controllable targets — this feeds both the plan and later renewal conversations.

**Questions to ask (MI-flavored — file 02):**
- "What made you decide to start now?" (the trigger / real driver)
- "What would achieving this actually change in your life?" (values → durable motivation)
- "How will you know it's working — what would you see or feel?" (defines success)
- "What's worked and not worked for you before?" (history, avoid past traps)
- "On 0–10, how important is this to you right now?" (readiness / stage of change — file 01 §5)

**Then translate into:**
- **One primary outcome goal** (direction — e.g. run a half-marathon, lose 8kg, first HYROX) with an honest timeline.
- **Process goals** she controls (file 01 §7) — the daily scoreboard.
- **The "why" documented** — reconnect to it when motivation dips (file 02).

LFG goal spread to map to: fat loss, body recomposition, strength, running (5k→half), HYROX, general fitness. Clarify which *one* is primary — chasing all at once dilutes everything.

## 4. Lifestyle audit (so the plan fits her real life)

The best plan she won't do is worthless. Audit the constraints and design around them (Fogg ability lever, file 01). Capture:

- **Schedule reality:** work hours, travel, kids/family duties, realistic training windows and *how many* days are genuinely available. (Time is the #1 stated dropout reason — build for her actual availability, not the ideal.)
- **Sleep:** average hours, quality — a foundational recovery and adherence variable.
- **Stress load:** work + life; is she near burnout? (affects programming aggressiveness)
- **Nutrition baseline:** typical eating pattern, meal timing, cooking vs. eating out, halal context, social/family food culture, alcohol, hydration (critical in Dubai heat).
- **Activity baseline:** current step count / general activity outside training.
- **Environment & access:** home vs. gym vs. outdoor; equipment; comfort in mixed vs. female-only spaces (modesty).
- **Support & barriers:** who supports her, who/what sabotages (file 01 §6 environment design).
- **GCC context:** Ramadan timing in the program window; fasting practices; hot-season heat; privacy/modesty preferences; any observance to schedule around.

> **Copilot rule:** Every prescribed plan must be checked against the lifestyle audit — training days ≤ genuinely available days, sessions sized to her real windows, nutrition fitting her actual food culture, and GCC filters (Ramadan/heat/modesty) applied.

## 5. Movement & fitness baselines (what to test)

Assess current capability to (a) program safely, (b) create measurable baselines, and (c) build competence with visible improvement later. Match testing to her goals and keep it *appropriate to her level* — don't max-test a deconditioned beginner.

**Movement screen (quality before load):**
- Basic movement competency: bodyweight squat, hinge, push, pull, lunge, overhead reach — watch for pain, restriction, or compensation.
- Note pain (→ refer, file 05) vs. mobility limitation (→ program around/improve).
- Balance and core control as relevant.

**Fitness baselines (pick by goal):**
- **Strength:** rep-max or working weights on key lifts (as level allows).
- **Cardio/running:** a baseline run (time/distance she can currently do — e.g. can she run 2k continuous?), resting HR.
- **HYROX-specific:** benchmark relevant stations/movements if that's the goal.
- **General fitness:** simple field tests (push-up count, plank, a timed walk/run).
- **Body composition baselines:** see file 07 (measurements, weight trend, and photos-or-alternatives *only with consent and modesty respected*).

**Log everything** as the week-0 baseline — this is what all progress is measured against and what makes renewal conversations concrete (file 03 §7).

## 6. What to measure at intake (the baseline checklist)

The copilot's intake data set to ensure the coach captures:

- [ ] **Readiness screen** completed (PAR-Q+ style) + any clearance obtained.
- [ ] **Health history** (conditions, injuries, meds noted, family history, pregnancy/postpartum status).
- [ ] **Red-flag check** done → refer if triggered.
- [ ] **Primary goal** + honest timeline + documented "why."
- [ ] **Process goals** defined and confidence-checked (≥9/10, file 01).
- [ ] **Lifestyle audit** (schedule, sleep, stress, nutrition, activity, environment, support, GCC context).
- [ ] **Movement screen** results.
- [ ] **Fitness baselines** matched to goal.
- [ ] **Body-comp baselines** (weight trend start, measurements; photos only with consent + modesty; offer alternatives).
- [ ] **Tracking method agreed** (what/how often — file 07) with modesty/privacy respected.
- [ ] **Expectations aligned** (realistic pace, scale-fluctuation reality, first-results-are-non-scale — file 03 §3).
- [ ] **Onboarding retention steps** triggered (file 03 §3): welcome, session-1-within-48h, community invite, day-14 review flagged.

> **Copilot intake summary rule:** After intake, produce a one-screen client profile — goal + why, constraints, baselines, any refer-out flags, agreed process goals, and tracking method — so every future recommendation can be checked against it. Nothing gets programmed until the readiness gate and red-flag check are clear.
