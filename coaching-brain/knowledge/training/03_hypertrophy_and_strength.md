# 03 — Hypertrophy & Strength

## The one-paragraph model
**Muscle grows from mechanical tension applied through a challenging load across a full range of motion, accumulated as enough hard sets, progressed over time, and recovered from.** Strength is largely muscle **plus** neuromuscular skill: practicing heavy, specific lifts so the nervous system fires the muscle you built more efficiently. So: build the muscle broadly, then express it as strength by practicing the specific lift heavy. Hypertrophy and strength overlap heavily; they differ mainly in load emphasis, proximity to failure, rest, and specificity.

## HYPERTROPHY

### Sets / reps / load / rest
- **Load:** muscle grows across ~**30–85% 1RM** *if sets are taken close to failure*. There is no single "hypertrophy rep range." (Schoenfeld load meta-analyses.)
- **Reps (practical):**
  - **5–8 reps** — heavy compounds, strength-biased hypertrophy, joint-friendly big movers.
  - **8–15 reps** — the bread-and-butter hypertrophy range for most work.
  - **15–30 reps** — isolation and lighter-load-tolerant muscles (side delts, calves, some quad/hamstring work); useful around cranky joints.
- **Proximity to failure:** most sets at **0–3 RIR**. Compound barbell work rarely to true failure (fatigue cost); isolation can go to 0–1 RIR safely.
- **Sets (volume):** per file 02 landmarks — start near MEV (~8–10/muscle/week), progress toward MAV/MRV (~12–20+), then deload.
- **Rest:** **~2–3 min for compounds**, **~1–2 min for isolation.** Longer rest preserves reps and total volume (which drives growth) — do NOT cut rest to make lifting "harder"; that just lowers quality volume. (Schoenfeld rest-interval work: longer rest ≥ short rest for hypertrophy.)
- **Frequency:** each muscle ≥2×/week when volume is meaningful, mainly to spread volume across quality sessions (file 01).
- **Tempo:** control the eccentric (~1–3 s), don't dawdle. Extreme super-slow tempos reduce load/volume without extra benefit. Full ROM beats partials for most muscles (deep stretch under load is a strong stimulus).

### Exercise selection logic (hypertrophy)
Pick exercises by **stimulus-to-fatigue ratio (SFR)**, coverage of the muscle, and what the client can do pain-free and repeat.
1. **Cover the muscle's functions/regions.** E.g. back needs a vertical pull (lats) + horizontal pull (mid-back) + a shrug/upper-trap element. Quads need a squat pattern + often a knee-extension. Delts need pressing + dedicated side- and rear-delt isolation.
2. **1–2 "anchor" compounds per muscle** (higher load, high stimulus) + **isolation to fill gaps** and target lagging areas with low systemic cost.
3. **Prefer high-SFR choices for volume:** machines and cables (chest press, hack squat, leg curl, cable row, lateral-raise machine) give strong local stimulus with low joint/systemic fatigue — ideal for the bulk of hypertrophy volume, especially for fat-loss clients with reduced recovery.
4. **Reserve high-fatigue lifts** (heavy barbell squats/deadlifts) for people who want those lifts or need the pattern; they're not mandatory for growth.
5. **Emphasize the loaded stretch:** exercises that challenge the muscle in its lengthened position (RDLs for hamstrings, incline curls for biceps, overhead extensions for triceps, deep hack squats) appear to be strong hypertrophy drivers.

### Technique cues (quick reference)
- **Squat:** brace, break at hips and knees together, knees track over toes, depth to at least parallel if mobility allows, drive through the whole foot.
- **RDL/hinge:** soft knees, push hips back, flat back, bar/DBs close to legs, feel hamstring stretch, stand tall by driving hips forward.
- **Bench:** shoulder blades retracted and down, slight arch, bar to lower chest, elbows ~45–75°, controlled touch.
- **Row:** torso stable, pull to lower ribs/belly, drive elbows back, squeeze, control the return.
- **Overhead press:** ribs down (no big lean-back), press slightly back so bar finishes over mid-foot, full lockout.
- **Universal:** control the eccentric, full ROM you can own, keep the target muscle doing the work (mind-muscle connection matters more on isolation).

### Progression models (hypertrophy)
- **Double progression (default):** fix a rep range (e.g. 3×8–12). When you hit the top of the range on all sets with good form, add the smallest load and drop back toward the bottom. Repeat. Simple, robust, works for compounds and isolation.
- **Volume progression (mesocycle):** add ~1–3 sets/muscle/week from MEV toward MRV, then deload (file 02). Layer on top of double progression within sessions.
- **Load progression:** on big movers where you can add weight, add small increments weekly and let reps recover.
- **Reps-in-reserve tightening:** across a block, the same sessions get harder as RIR drifts from ~3 toward ~0–1 before the deload.

## STRENGTH

### Sets / reps / load / rest
- **Load/reps:** **≥80–90% 1RM, ~1–5 reps** on main lifts for maximal strength; **6–10 reps** for strength-oriented accessories. Skill of heavy lifting requires actually lifting heavy.
- **Proximity to failure:** top sets **1–4 RIR** — leave reps in the tank on barbell compounds. Grinding maximal singles to failure repeatedly buys fatigue and injury risk, not strength.
- **Sets:** typically **3–6 working sets** on a main lift per session; total weekly hard sets per lift managed like volume (moderate, quality over quantity).
- **Rest:** **~3–5 min** between heavy compound sets — full recovery of the nervous system and phosphocreatine so each set is high quality. This is not optional for strength.
- **Frequency:** the more you practice a heavy lift (as a skill), the better — **2–4×/week per main lift** for dedicated strength athletes; general clients can build strength on 2×.

### Exercise selection logic (strength)
Strength is specific (SAID). Selection is narrower than hypertrophy:
1. **Train the competition/goal lift itself** (or its closest variation) frequently. Want a bigger squat → squat, plus close variations (pause, tempo, front, box) to address weak points.
2. **Variations target weak points and manage fatigue:** e.g. pause squats for the hole, deficit deadlifts for the floor, close-grip/spoto bench for lockout/chest speed.
3. **Accessories build the muscle that underlies the lift** (hypertrophy work at 6–12 reps for the prime movers and supporting muscles). Bigger muscle = higher strength ceiling.
4. **Manage systemic fatigue:** you can't do maximal squats, deadlifts, and everything else fresh; rotate emphasis (DUP, block) so not everything is heavy every day.

### Progression models (strength)
- **Linear progression (novices):** add a small fixed load each session (e.g. +2.5 kg upper / +5 kg lower) until you stall, then reset ~10% and climb again, or switch models. The fastest phase — don't waste it with fancy periodization.
- **Double progression:** e.g. 4×4–6; when 4×6 hits at target RPE, add load, drop to 4×4.
- **Percentage-based / block:** run % of 1RM across blocks getting heavier (SBS-style 7-week blocks, deload at the end), peaking toward a test.
- **RPE-based top set + back-offs:** work to a top set at target RPE, back-offs at a % of that (autoregulates to daily readiness). Best for intermediates/advanced (Nuckols/Tuchscherer).
- **DUP within the week:** a heavy day (3–5 @ RPE 8), a volume day (6–8 @ RPE 7), a lighter/speed day — keeps strength and size progressing while spreading fatigue.

### When strength stalls (order of operations)
1. Check recovery (sleep, food, stress) — most "plateaus" are recovery, not programming.
2. Deload one week, then re-approach the load.
3. Reset the load ~10% and re-climb (novice), or switch to autoregulated top-set/back-off (intermediate).
4. Add variation work for the specific sticking point.
5. Add muscle: if the prime movers are simply too small, run a hypertrophy block, then re-peak strength.

## How hypertrophy and strength fit together
- **They're synergistic, not opposed.** Bigger muscle raises the strength ceiling; heavier training teaches you to use it. Most general clients want both.
- **Sequence for a goal:** hypertrophy/accumulation block (more reps, more volume, build tissue) → strength/intensification block (heavier, lower reps, express it) → optional peak/test. This is block periodization applied to a mixed goal.
- **For a general-fitness/fat-loss female client** wanting to "tone and get stronger": this is just hypertrophy + a bit of strength — 6–12 reps, progressive overload, 2–3× frequency per muscle, get demonstrably stronger on a handful of lifts over months. "Toned" = more muscle + less fat (file 04); it is not a separate training method.

## Cheat sheet
- **Grow:** 6–15 reps mostly, 0–3 RIR, 2–3 min rest, full ROM + loaded stretch, ~10–20 hard sets/muscle/week, ≥2×/week, double progression.
- **Get strong:** 1–5 reps on main lifts, ≥80% 1RM, 1–4 RIR, 3–5 min rest, practice the lift 2–4×/week, autoregulate top sets.
- **Pick exercises** by stimulus-to-fatigue ratio, muscle coverage, and pain-free repeatability. No exercise is mandatory; the pattern is.
- **Rest longer than feels necessary** — quality volume drives both goals; cutting rest to feel the burn lowers the stimulus.
- **Stalled → fix recovery → deload → reset/autoregulate → add variation → add muscle.**
