# 02 — Program Design (End to End)

**Sources synthesized:** Eric Helms — *The Muscle & Strength Pyramids: Training* (program hierarchy, autoregulation, RIR); Renaissance Periodization / Mike Israetel (volume landmarks, mesocycle structure, deloads); Greg Nuckols / Stronger By Science (periodization evidence, block structure, RPE/RTF); Brad Schoenfeld (volume & frequency dose-response); NSCA *Essentials of Strength & Conditioning* (needs analysis, periodization models); Mladen Jovanović / Mike Tuchscherer (RPE/RIR autoregulation). Original synthesis, frameworks cited.

---

## The design pipeline (follow in order)
1. **Needs analysis** — who is this, what do they want, what can they do?
2. **Goal → template mapping** — pick the archetype that fits the primary goal.
3. **Split selection** — distribute the work across the available days.
4. **Volume landmarks** — set starting weekly sets per muscle / weekly km.
5. **Progression + periodization** — how load/volume moves week to week.
6. **Deload + autoregulation** — how to manage fatigue and daily readiness.
7. **Exercise selection** — fill the slots (logic in file 03).

Never skip step 1. A program is a solution to a specific person's problem, not a template you paste.

---

## 1. Needs analysis (the intake the AI must run)
Gather and record before programming:

- **Primary goal** (rank if several): fat loss, recomp, hypertrophy, strength, run a 5k/10k/half, HYROX/hybrid, general health.
- **Training age / status:** beginner (<~6–12 mo consistent), intermediate (~1–3 yr), advanced (3+ yr, near genetic ceiling for the lift).
- **Days available & session length:** 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 days? 30, 45, 60, 90 min?
- **Equipment:** full gym / home dumbbells / bands / bodyweight / access to a track or treadmill. (Dubai heat means outdoor running is seasonal/early-morning — default treadmill or dawn runs May–Sept.)
- **Injury history & current pain** (file 07): knees, low back, shoulders, prior surgeries, pregnancy/postpartum status.
- **Recovery context:** sleep, job stress, kids, current nutrition, dieting or not.
- **Preferences / dislikes:** what they'll actually adhere to.
- **Baseline capacity:** current lifts, current weekly running volume, current conditioning.

**Output:** a one-paragraph client profile the rest of the design references.

## 2. Goal → template mapping

| Primary goal | Training emphasis | Weekly structure default | Rep/intensity bias | Cardio/conditioning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fat loss** | Retain muscle in a deficit | Full-body 3× or Upper/Lower 4× | 6–15 reps, 1–3 RIR | Zone 2 + some intervals; steps target (file 04) |
| **Recomp** | Build muscle at maintenance/slight deficit | Upper/Lower 4× or PPL-derived | 6–15 reps, 1–3 RIR | Moderate; don't sabotage recovery (file 04) |
| **Hypertrophy** | Maximize muscle | Upper/Lower 4× or PPL 5–6× | 6–15 reps, 0–3 RIR | Minimal/optional (file 03) |
| **Strength** | Maximize force on key lifts | Upper/Lower or full-body strength 3–4× | 1–6 reps main, 6–10 accessory, 1–4 RIR | Low, kept easy to protect recovery |
| **5k–half marathon** | Aerobic base + specific pace | 3–5 runs + 2 lifts (file 05) | Lifting 5–8 reps low volume | Running IS the training |
| **HYROX/hybrid** | Strength-endurance + running | 4–6 sessions, concurrent (file 06) | Mixed; strength-endurance emphasis | Compromised running + stations |
| **General health** | Do a bit of everything | Full-body 3× + 2 easy cardio | 8–15 reps, 2–3 RIR | 150 min/wk moderate cardio |

## 3. Split selection (distribute the volume)
Pick the split that fits **days available** and lets each muscle be hit ~2×/week.

- **2 days/week → Full-body A/B.** Every session covers a squat pattern, a hinge, a horizontal push/pull, a vertical push/pull, and a core piece. Best for busy beginners and maintenance.
- **3 days/week → Full-body A/B/C** (default for most general-fitness and fat-loss clients) or an Upper/Lower/Full. Highest bang-for-buck for beginners.
- **4 days/week → Upper/Lower ×2** (the workhorse for intermediates: recomp, hypertrophy, strength) or Upper/Lower/Push/Pull.
- **5 days/week → Upper/Lower + PPL hybrid,** or a body-part-focused split for advanced hypertrophy.
- **6 days/week → Push/Pull/Legs ×2** for advanced hypertrophy only, or distribute a hybrid/HYROX schedule (file 06).

**Rule:** frequency exists to fit weekly volume in with good per-session quality, not for its own sake. If 3 days covers the client's needed volume with 2× frequency per muscle, don't add a 4th day just to add a day.

### Full-body 3× template (movement-pattern based)
Each day: **1 lower push (squat), 1 lower pull (hinge), 1 upper push, 1 upper pull, 1 core/accessory.** Rotate the specific exercise across A/B/C so patterns are trained ~3× but from varied angles.
- Day A: Back squat / RDL / bench / row / plank
- Day B: Front squat or leg press / hip thrust / overhead press / lat pulldown / carry
- Day C: Goblet/hack squat / deadlift or good morning / incline DB press / cable row / side plank

### Upper/Lower 4× template
- **Upper A (push bias):** bench, OHP, row, lateral raise, triceps, biceps
- **Lower A (squat bias):** squat, leg press, leg curl, calf, core
- **Upper B (pull bias):** pull-up/pulldown, incline press, row, rear delt, biceps, triceps
- **Lower B (hinge bias):** RDL/deadlift, hip thrust, leg extension, calf, core

## 4. Weekly volume landmarks (starting doses)
Use **hard sets per muscle group per week** (RP model + Schoenfeld dose-response, file 01). Start intermediates in the MEV–MAV band and progress up across the mesocycle.

| Muscle group | MV (hold) | MEV (start) | MAV (grow) | MRV (ceiling) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 4–6 | 8–10 | 12–20 | ~22 |
| Back | 6–8 | 10 | 14–22 | ~25 |
| Quads | 4–6 | 8–10 | 12–18 | ~20 |
| Hamstrings | 3–4 | 6–8 | 10–16 | ~18 |
| Glutes | 0–4 | 4–8 | 8–16 | ~16+ |
| Shoulders (side/rear delts) | 6 | 8 | 16–22 | ~26 |
| Biceps | 4 | 6–8 | 12–20 | ~20 |
| Triceps | 4 | 6–8 | 10–18 | ~18 |
| Calves | 6 | 8 | 12–16 | ~20 |

Rules:
- **Beginners:** start at MEV or below (~6–10 sets/muscle) — they grow on little and shouldn't bury themselves in fatigue.
- **Deficit / fat loss:** program nearer **MV–MEV**; recovery is reduced, so hold muscle rather than chase growth (file 04).
- **A "hard set"** = a working set within ~0–4 RIR. Warm-ups don't count.
- **Progress volume, don't start maxed.** Begin a block near MEV, add ~1–3 sets/muscle/week toward MAV/MRV, then deload.

## 5. Periodization (organize the timeline)
Periodization = planned variation of volume, intensity, and specificity over time. Evidence (Nuckols / Stronger By Science *"Periodization: What the Data Say"*): **any structured periodization beats none; no single model is clearly superior for hypertrophy when volume and effort are matched.** Choose the model that best organizes progression and fatigue for the client.

- **Linear periodization:** volume high/intensity low early, shifting to intensity high/volume low over weeks. Simple; good for peaking a strength client toward a test.
- **Undulating (daily/weekly, DUP):** vary rep ranges within the week (e.g. a heavy 3–5, a moderate 6–8, a lighter 10–12 day per lift). Great for intermediates; keeps all qualities trained and manages joint fatigue.
- **Block periodization:** sequential blocks each emphasizing one quality (accumulation/hypertrophy → intensification/strength → peak). The SBS programs use 7-week blocks getting progressively heavier with a deload at the end of each. Best for advanced athletes and event peaking.

### The mesocycle (the practical unit — 4–6 weeks)
This is how most LFG hypertrophy/recomp blocks run (RP-style):
- **Week 1:** start near MEV, ~2–3 RIR. Establish loads and technique.
- **Weeks 2–4(–5):** add ~1–3 sets/muscle/week and/or small loads; RIR drifts from ~3 down toward ~0–1 as fatigue accumulates and you ride volume toward MRV.
- **Final week (deload):** cut sets ~40–60%, reduce load ~10–20% (keep bar speed), let fatigue clear.
- **Repeat**, starting the next block a notch higher than the last.

Then several mesocycles chain into a **macrocycle** aimed at a goal date (a race, a photoshoot, a strength test).

---

## 6. Deloads
A planned light week to shed accumulated fatigue and realize adaptation (file 01, §4).
- **When:** every ~4–8 weeks (sooner for advanced/high-volume or dieting clients; later for beginners who barely need them), or reactively when performance drops 2+ sessions in a row with recovery otherwise fine.
- **How:** keep frequency and movement, drop **volume ~50%** and/or **intensity ~10–20%**, stay 3–5 RIR. Not a week off — a week easy. Bar speed should feel fast.
- **Endurance deload:** cut weekly volume ~30–50%, keep one short quality touch, skip the long run (file 05).

---

## 7. Autoregulation (RPE / RIR)
Autoregulation adjusts the daily dose to the client's actual readiness instead of a rigid number. Evidence supports subjective (RPE/RIR) and objective (velocity) methods for tuning intensity/volume day to day (Helms; Nuckols; autoregulation reviews).

**RIR (Reps in Reserve)** — how many good reps you had left in the tank:
- **0 RIR** = failure. **1–2 RIR** = 1–2 reps left (typical hard hypertrophy set). **3–4 RIR** = several left (technique/volume work, deloads).

**RPE (10-point, RIR-based mapping):** RPE 10 = 0 RIR; RPE 9 = 1 RIR; RPE 8 = 2 RIR; RPE 7 = 3 RIR.

Practical autoregulation methods the AI can prescribe:
- **Top set + back-offs (RTS-style):** work up to a top set at a target RPE (e.g. RPE 8 on squats), then do back-off sets at a % of that top load for volume. On a bad day the top-set load self-lowers; on a good day it rises.
- **RIR-capped volume:** prescribe sets "until form breaks / RIR hits target," within a min–max set range. Stop the session's added sets when quality drops.
- **Readiness check-in:** if sleep/stress/soreness are poor, drop to the low end of the set range and add ~1 RIR (train lighter, still train).

**Rules:**
- Hypertrophy: most sets **1–3 RIR**; occasional failure on isolations only.
- Strength: top sets **1–4 RIR**, rarely to failure on barbell compounds (fatigue/injury cost too high).
- Beginners: keep **2–3 RIR** and prioritize technique — they can't yet gauge failure accurately, so don't push them there.

## Ready-to-use template structures

### A) Fat-loss / recomp — Upper/Lower 4× (intermediate)
- **Upper A:** Bench 3×6–10 · 1-arm DB row 3×8–12 · Incline DB press 3×10–12 · Lat pulldown 3×10–12 · Lateral raise 3×12–20 · Triceps pushdown 2×12–15
- **Lower A:** Back squat 3×5–8 · RDL 3×8–10 · Leg press 3×10–15 · Leg curl 3×10–15 · Calf raise 3×12–20
- **Upper B:** OHP 3×6–10 · Pull-up/pulldown 3×8–12 · Chest-supported row 3×10–12 · Cable fly 2×12–15 · Rear-delt fly 3×15–20 · Biceps curl 3×10–15
- **Lower B:** Hip thrust 3×8–12 · Front squat or hack 3×8–12 · Walking lunge 2×10–12/leg · Leg extension 3×12–15 · Calf raise 3×12–20 · Core 3 sets
- **Conditioning:** 2–3× Zone-2 (20–40 min) + optional 1 short interval session; daily step target (file 04). Progress via double progression; deload wk 5–6.

### B) Hypertrophy — PPL 6× (advanced only)
- Push (chest/shoulders/triceps) · Pull (back/rear delt/biceps) · Legs, run twice. Each muscle ~14–20 sets/week riding MEV→MRV over 4–5 weeks, then deload. Most sets 1–2 RIR.

### C) Strength — Upper/Lower 4× (intermediate, DUP)
- **Lower A (heavy):** Squat 4×3–5 @ RPE 7–8 · Deadlift 3×3–5 · accessories 8–10 reps
- **Upper A (heavy):** Bench 4×3–5 @ RPE 7–8 · Row 4×5–8 · OHP 3×6–8 · accessories
- **Lower B (volume):** Squat 4×6–8 @ RPE 7 · RDL 3×6–8 · accessories 10–12
- **Upper B (volume):** Bench 4×6–8 · Pull-up 4×AMRAP-2 · accessories 10–15
- Progress the top-set load when RPE comes in below target; deload every 4–6 weeks.

### D) General health / true beginner — Full-body 3×
- Goblet squat 3×8–12 · DB RDL 3×8–12 · DB bench or push-up 3×8–12 · DB row 3×8–12 · plank 3× · finish with 15–20 min easy cardio. Linear progression: add reps then load. Rotate exercise variations across A/B/C.

## Program-design heuristics (cheat sheet)
- **Fewer days than volume needs → shorten sessions, don't skip the goal muscles; pick higher-SFR exercises.**
- **In a deficit → program at MV–MEV, keep intensity, cut volume, not effort.**
- **Intermediate plateau → add periodization (DUP) and autoregulation before adding raw volume.**
- **Adherence risk → simpler split, fewer exercises, double progression.**
- **Always attach: a progression rule + a deload trigger + an RIR target to every block.**
