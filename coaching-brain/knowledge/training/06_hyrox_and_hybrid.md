## What HYROX actually is (train the demand — SAID)
A HYROX race = **8 × 1 km runs, each followed by one functional station**, in this fixed order:
1. 1 km run → **SkiErg** (1000 m)
2. 1 km run → **Sled Push** (heavy)
3. 1 km run → **Sled Pull** (heavy)
4. 1 km run → **Burpee Broad Jumps** (80 m)
5. 1 km run → **Rowing** (1000 m)
6. 1 km run → **Farmers Carry** (200 m)
7. 1 km run → **Sandbag Lunges** (100 m)
8. 1 km run → **Wall Balls** (75/100 reps)

**Key implication:** it's ~50% running (≈8 km total) done in a **fatigued, "compromised" state** — you never run fresh. The defining skill is **holding running pace and form after a taxing station, repeatedly.** So the training must rehearse *compromised running* and *strength-endurance under fatigue*, not just raw strength or fresh running.

Dominant physical qualities: **aerobic capacity (biggest), running economy under fatigue, muscular/strength-endurance (legs, posterior chain, grip, shoulders), lactate tolerance, and pacing.** Max strength matters far less than the ability to repeat sub-maximal efforts.

---

## The central problem: the interference effect
Training strength and endurance concurrently can blunt each other — the **interference effect** (Hickson 1980; Wilson et al. meta-analysis). Endurance work (especially high volume/high intensity running) can attenuate strength and hypertrophy gains via competing signaling and residual fatigue. It runs **mostly one direction**: heavy endurance interferes with strength more than lifting interferes with endurance.

For HYROX this is unavoidable — the sport *requires* both. The goal isn't to eliminate interference; it's to **manage** it so both qualities develop enough for the event. HYROX's own programming philosophy is explicitly built to "moderate interference between strength and endurance to maximize performance."

### How to minimize interference (encode these)
1. **Separate the stressors.** Put hard running and heavy lifting on different days, or hours apart, when possible. Don't do max squats and a threshold run back-to-back if you can avoid it.
2. **If same session, order by priority of the day.** Do the quality you're emphasizing first while fresh (usually: strength-endurance/lifting before easy running, or hard running before easy lifting — never two maximal stimuli stacked cold).
3. **Keep most running EASY (80/20, file 05).** Easy aerobic volume builds the base with minimal interference; reserve hard running for a couple of targeted sessions. Grey-zone running is the worst offender — high fatigue, high interference, low return.
4. **Emphasize strength-ENDURANCE over max strength.** Higher-rep, repeatable, sub-maximal work (which the event demands) interferes far less than heavy 1–5 rep maximal lifting — and is more specific anyway.
5. **Fuel and recover.** Interference worsens under-fueled and under-slept; adequate carbs and sleep blunt it.
6. **Periodize emphasis.** Off-season can bias one quality (e.g. a strength/hypertrophy base block with maintenance running), then shift specific as the race nears.

## Blending strength + endurance in one plan

### The four ingredients every HYROX week needs
1. **Aerobic base** — mostly easy running (+ ski/row/bike as low-impact aerobic volume).
2. **Compromised running** — running immediately after a station, at race effort (the specific skill).
3. **Strength-endurance** — repeatable work on the event's patterns: lunges, wall balls (squat-to-press), sled push/pull, carries, hinge, rowing/ski power-endurance, burpees.
4. **Some strength / power base** — moderate heavy lifting (squat, hinge, press, pull) to raise the ceiling the endurance draws on — kept as a supporting, not dominant, element.

### Session types
- **Easy aerobic run / Zone-2 machine work** — base volume.
- **Threshold / interval run** — 1 hard running session (tempo or 1 km/800 m repeats near race pace) to lift running fitness.
- **Strength-endurance circuits** — station-pattern work for reps/time (e.g. EMOMs, intervals, "Rox"-style couplets).
- **Compromised-running "Rox" workouts** — alternate runs with stations, e.g. 4 × (400–1000 m run + one station) at target effort. The most specific and most important as the race nears.
- **Strength day** — squat/hinge/press/pull, moderate loads, 4–8 reps, not to failure, to maintain/build the base.

## Sample weekly structures

### 3 days/week (minimum effective dose, beginner/time-poor)
- **Day 1 — Aerobic + strength-endurance:** easy run 20–30 min + station circuit (lunges, wall balls, row) for quality/reps.
- **Day 2 — Strength:** squat, hinge, press, pull (4–8 reps) + carries/grip.
- **Day 3 — Compromised-running "Rox":** 4–6 × (600–1000 m run + a station) at race-ish effort.
- Keep runs mostly easy; one session carries the hard running.

### 4 days/week (the sweet spot for most first-timers)
- **Day 1 — Strength** (squat/press focus) + short easy run.
- **Day 2 — Aerobic:** easy/long run (or Zone-2 machine) + strides.
- **Day 3 — Strength-endurance circuits** (station patterns for reps/time).
- **Day 4 — Compromised "Rox" simulation** (runs + stations alternating) or a threshold run.
- Separate hard running from heavy lifting across the week to limit interference.

### 5–6 days/week (experienced hybrid athlete)
- Add a second easy aerobic day and/or a dedicated threshold/interval run, keeping ~80% of running easy. Distribute: 2 strength, 1 strength-endurance, 1 compromised-run, 2 aerobic (one long). Watch cumulative fatigue; deload every 4–6 weeks.

**Progression toward a race:** general base (build both qualities, more easy volume, moderate strength) → specific block (more compromised-running and station simulations at race effort, running biased to specific paces) → sharpen/taper (cut volume ~30–40% the last ~1–2 weeks, keep race-pace touches, arrive fresh).

## Station-specific pointers
- **Running (biggest lever):** most points are won/lost here. Build easy aerobic volume + pace it conservatively early in the race; practice running the first km *after* a hard station so you learn to settle fast.
- **Wall balls (often the killer at the end):** high-rep squat-to-press endurance — train unbroken sets under fatigue and pacing (break before you fail, not after).
- **Sleds (push/pull):** heavy horizontal leg drive; train with heavy sled work + strong quads/glutes and low posture; grip for the pull.
- **Lunges (sandbag) & carries (farmers):** loaded single-leg endurance and grip/trunk endurance — trained with weighted walking lunges and heavy carries for distance.
- **SkiErg / Row:** power-endurance and technique/efficiency; practice pacing 1000 m efforts and the transition into running.
- **Burpee broad jumps:** full-body endurance and hip power under fatigue; practice a smooth, repeatable rhythm.
- **Grip** is a hidden limiter (sleds, carries, ski/row) — train it directly (carries, holds).

## Hybrid (non-HYROX) athletes
Same principles for anyone chasing "strong AND can run" (a common LFG ask):
- Decide the **priority** and bias volume toward it; keep the other as maintenance.
- Separate hard sessions; keep most running easy; lift 2–4×/week with moderate volume.
- Accept slightly submaximal results in each quality — that's the price of doing both; a true hybrid won't match a specialist in either, and that's fine.
- Manage total fatigue: fewer truly-hard sessions per week than a specialist, more easy aerobic filler.

---

## HYROX / hybrid cheat sheet
- **Train the specific demand: ~50% running done tired, plus repeatable strength-endurance — not max strength or fresh running.**
- **Interference is real and mostly one-way (endurance blunts strength). Manage it: separate stressors, keep 80% of running easy, bias strength-endurance over max strength, fuel and sleep.**
- **4 days/week is the sweet spot for most first-timers:** ~2 strength/strength-endurance, ~1–2 running (mostly easy), 1 compromised-run simulation.
- **Running is the biggest lever; wall balls and sleds are common late-race killers; grip is a hidden limiter.**
- **Periodize: base → specific (compromised-run simulations) → taper.**
