## PART A — WOMEN'S TRAINING CONSIDERATIONS

### Baseline: women train on the same principles
Women build muscle and strength through the same mechanisms as men (tension, overload, volume, progression). They gain strength and relative muscle at comparable rates; absolute kg gains are smaller (less total muscle mass). **Do not "shrink" women's programs** — the biggest error is under-dosing women with light weights and endless reps. Train them to get genuinely strong: same rep ranges, real progressive overload, compound lifts. "Toning" = building muscle + losing fat (file 04), not a separate method.

### Menstrual cycle
**Current evidence does NOT support mandatory cycle-phase periodization of strength.** Systematic reviews and recent consensus (Colenso-Semple; Frontiers 2023) find menstrual-cycle phase has, at most, trivial and inconsistent effects on strength performance and training adaptation — prior "train harder in the follicular phase" claims rest on weak methodology.

Practical stance for the AI:
- **Program by consistent principles, not by phase.** Don't build a rigid follicular/luteal periodization as if it's established science.
- **Individualize around symptoms, not the calendar.** Some women feel worse (fatigue, cramps, low energy, poor sleep) around menstruation or in the late-luteal/PMS window. On those days, **autoregulate**: use RIR/RPE (file 02) to reduce load/volume, or swap a hard session for easy work — then resume normal programming. This is symptom-management, not phase-periodization.
- **Track if the client wants to** (symptoms vs. training) to spot personal patterns — but treat it as n-of-1 individualization, not a universal template.
- **Iron/energy availability:** heavy periods can contribute to low iron/fatigue; persistent unusual fatigue → refer for bloodwork. Under-fueling + hard training can cause menstrual dysfunction (see RED-S below).

### RED-S / low energy availability (important guardrail)
Chronic under-fueling relative to training (common in fat-loss-focused female clients) can cause **Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S)**: menstrual irregularity/loss, poor recovery, stalled performance, low bone density, mood/sleep issues. **Loss or irregularity of the menstrual cycle is a red flag, not a goal** — raise energy intake, reduce excessive training, and **refer to a physician.** Don't push aggressive deficits on already-lean, high-volume female clients.

### Pregnancy (training modifications — coach within medical clearance)
Follow ACOG: for those with an uncomplicated pregnancy and medical clearance, **regular moderate exercise is safe and beneficial** — aim ~150 min/week moderate activity, and resistance training can continue.
- **Continue what she's adapted to, modify intensity down over time.** Not the time to start a brand-new maximal-strength push or chase PRs.
- **Modifications:** avoid supine (flat-on-back) positions for prolonged periods after ~first trimester; avoid contact/fall-risk activities, heavy Valsalva/max straining, and overheating (very relevant in Dubai — keep cool, hydrate). Use RPE, keep able to talk.
- **Core:** shift from crunches/heavy flexion to breathing, pelvic-floor, and anti-movement core work as pregnancy progresses.
- **RED-FLAG → stop and seek care:** vaginal bleeding, fluid leakage, regular painful contractions, dizziness, chest pain, calf pain/swelling, headache, reduced fetal movement. **All programming stays inside the OB-GYN's clearance.** Refer for anything beyond routine.

### Postpartum
- **No fixed "6-week and you're cleared" rule** — 6 weeks is a *minimum* checkpoint, not a green light for impact. Return is gradual and individualized.
- **First weeks:** breathing, gentle pelvic-floor work (once comfortable), walking, posture, and reconnecting core — pelvic-floor training in the first year cuts incontinence and prolapse risk substantially.
- **Return to running:** guidelines (Goom et al.) suggest **~3+ months postpartum minimum** and ideally assessment by a **pelvic-floor physiotherapist** first, checking for pelvic-floor dysfunction, diastasis recti, load tolerance (e.g. single-leg work, hopping) before impact. Don't rush impact.
- **Diastasis recti / leaking / heaviness/prolapse symptoms / pain → refer to a pelvic-floor PT.** Avoid aggressive crunching, heavy straining, and high-impact until cleared and symptom-free.
- **Rebuild slowly:** strength before impact, low-intensity before high, and progress by symptoms (no leaking, no doming, no pain).

### Menopause / perimenopause
Declining estrogen accelerates **muscle and bone loss** and shifts fat distribution — which makes **resistance training more important, not less.**
- **Prioritize resistance training** (2–4×/week) with progressive overload to defend muscle mass, strength, and metabolic health. Don't default menopausal women to "just cardio."
- **Include heavier/impact loading for bone** where safe (progressive resistance + weight-bearing/impact within tolerance) to support bone density — build up gradually, respect joints and history.
- **Protein higher** (≥~1.6–2.0+ g/kg) to offset anabolic resistance; recovery may need more attention (sleep, deloads).
- **Individualize around symptoms** (sleep disruption, joint aches, hot flashes, energy) via autoregulation. Medical management of menopause (HRT etc.) is a **physician** matter — stay in the training lane.

## PART B — OTHER POPULATIONS

### Beginners
- Prioritize **skill/technique and habit** over load. Start at **MEV or below** (~6–10 sets/muscle/week), full-body 3× (file 02), **linear progression** (file 01/03), **2–3 RIR** (don't train near failure — they can't yet judge it).
- Expect fast early progress ("newbie gains") — recomp is very achievable here (file 04).
- Keep it simple and repeatable; adherence and consistency beat optimization at this stage.

### Older adults
- Resistance training is **high-value and safe** with sensible scaling — it directly fights sarcopenia, protects bone, and preserves independence and balance.
- **Emphasize:** progressive resistance training 2–3×/week, plus **power** (moving submaximal loads with intent — protects against falls) and **balance** work.
- **Scale:** longer warm-ups, more gradual load progression, machines/supported variations where balance or joints demand, full recovery between sessions.
- Screen for cardiovascular/medical history; **medical clearance** for those with cardiac risk, uncontrolled BP, or relevant conditions. Refer when in doubt.

## PART C — COMMON INJURY MODIFICATIONS (train around, refer when needed)

**Universal rules:**
- **Pain scale:** low-level discomfort (≤~3/10) that doesn't worsen during or after can often be trained around; **sharp, worsening, or lingering/increasing pain → stop that movement and reassess/refer.**
- **Modify the movement, keep training the rest of the body.** An injury is rarely a reason to stop all training — it's a reason to change *which* exercises.
- **Find pain-free variations:** adjust range of motion, load, tempo, angle, grip/stance, or swap the exercise for one that trains the same muscle without provoking symptoms.
- **RED-FLAG → refer:** numbness/tingling/weakness/radiating pain (possible nerve), night pain, giving-way, locking, significant swelling, trauma/suspected fracture, or pain that won't settle. **Refer to a physiotherapist or physician.**

### Knee
- **Common issues:** patellofemoral ("runner's knee"), patellar tendinopathy, general anterior knee pain.
- **Modify:** reduce squat/lunge depth to a pain-free range; shift toward **hip-dominant** work (hip thrust, RDL, glute work) and controlled quad work (leg press/extension in a pain-free arc, tempo, isometrics — wall sits/Spanish squats are well-tolerated for many tendinopathies); reduce impact/running load if that's the driver.
- **Build:** progressive quad + glute strength is protective. Avoid pushing into sharp pain; heavy deep loading returns as symptoms settle.
- **Refer:** locking, giving-way, significant swelling, instability, or non-settling pain.

### Low back
- **Common issues:** non-specific low-back pain, flare-ups after heavy hinging/loading.
- **Modify:** avoid loaded end-range flexion under fatigue during a flare; emphasize a **braced neutral spine**, anti-movement core (planks, dead bugs, bird-dogs, Pallof press, carries), and hip-hinge patterning at a tolerable load; reduce deadlift/heavy-hinge load and rebuild gradually. Many tolerate trap-bar or elevated/rack-pull variations better than floor deadlifts during recovery.
- **Keep moving:** gentle movement and walking usually beat rest for non-specific back pain.
- **RED-FLAG → urgent referral:** leg weakness/numbness, radiating leg pain, or **bowel/bladder changes or saddle numbness (medical emergency — cauda equina)**; also trauma or unexplained progressive pain.

### Shoulder
- **Common issues:** impingement/rotator-cuff irritation, AC-joint niggles, pain with overhead pressing or bench.
- **Modify:** switch to **pain-free planes** — neutral-grip/DB pressing, landmine press, incline instead of flat/overhead; reduce ROM (floor press, board work) to a comfortable range; avoid deep-stretch positions (e.g. very low wide-grip bench, behind-neck) that provoke it.
- **Build:** rotator-cuff and scapular work (external rotations, face pulls, controlled rows, serratus/scapular control); heavy overhead work returns as symptoms allow.
- **Refer:** significant weakness, night pain, dislocation history, or non-settling pain (possible cuff tear).

### Running-related (cross-ref file 05)
- **Shin splints (MTSS), runner's knee, Achilles/calf, ITB, plantar fascia:** first-line is **load management** — reduce running volume/impact, correct the ramp (keep within ~0.8–1.3× the 4-week average), add relevant strength (calf/tibialis for shins; hip/glute for knee/ITB), consider a small cadence increase (+5–10%) for overstriders, and return gradually.
- **Refer:** focal bone pain or pain that persists at rest/night (rule out **stress fracture**), or any symptom that doesn't settle with sensible modification.

## PART D — EQUIPMENT & SCHEDULE LIMITS (safe scaling)
- **Home / minimal equipment:** train the same movement patterns with what's available — dumbbells, bands, and bodyweight cover it. Use higher reps, tempo, unilateral work, and shorter rest to make lighter loads challenging (double progression still applies). Bodyweight progressions (push-up → decline/archer, split squat → shrimp/pistol, rows via bands/rings) drive real progress.
- **Time-limited sessions:** cut isolation before compounds; use supersets/antagonist pairing to fit volume into less time; prioritize the goal muscles/lifts; drop frequency-for-its-own-sake, keep total weekly volume where it needs to be (file 02).
- **Travel / gaps:** a maintenance dose (MV, ~2 short full-body sessions) holds strength and muscle for weeks — reassure clients that a lighter week won't undo their progress.

## Special populations cheat sheet
- **Train women hard and progressively — same principles, real loads. "Toning" = build muscle + lose fat.**
- **No mandatory menstrual-cycle periodization — autoregulate around symptoms, not the calendar.**
- **Lost/irregular periods = red flag (RED-S/under-fueling) → raise intake, reduce load, refer.**
- **Pregnancy:** modify within OB-GYN clearance, don't start new maximal work, avoid overheating (Dubai), refer on any warning sign.
- **Postpartum:** gradual, pelvic-floor first, ~3+ months + ideally PT assessment before running; refer on leaking/diastasis/prolapse/pain.
- **Menopause:** prioritize resistance + impact for muscle and bone; more protein; individualize by symptoms.
- **Injuries:** modify the movement, keep training the rest, stay pain-free, build the supporting muscle — and **refer on any red flag or non-settling pain.**
- **When in doubt, refer to a medical professional. We coach; we don't diagnose or treat.**
