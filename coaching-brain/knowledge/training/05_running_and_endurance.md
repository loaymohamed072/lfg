# 05 — Running & Endurance (Beginner 5k → Half Marathon)

**Sources synthesized:** Jack Daniels — *Daniels' Running Formula* (VDOT, E/M/T/I/R paces, training zones); Stephen Seiler — polarized / 80/20 intensity distribution research; Matt Fitzgerald — *80/20 Running*; Jeff Galloway (run/walk method for beginners); load-management / acute:chronic workload research (Gabbett et al.); running-injury literature (medial tibial stress syndrome, cadence, load). Original synthesis; frameworks cited. Running is a stated LFG specialty.

---

## The one-paragraph model
**Endurance is built mostly by lots of easy aerobic running, seasoned with a small dose of hard quality work, progressed gradually enough that tissues adapt without breaking.** The two biggest mistakes recreational runners make: (1) running their easy days too hard (grey-zone "moderate" running that fatigues without building), and (2) ramping volume too fast (the main cause of running injuries). Fix those two and most runners improve and stay healthy.

---

## 1. Intensity distribution — the 80/20 rule
Across a week, **~80% of running should be genuinely EASY, ~20% moderate-to-hard.** (Seiler's polarized model; Daniels' emphasis on easy volume; Fitzgerald's 80/20.) Most amateurs run ~50–50 and get stuck.

- **Easy = conversational.** You can speak full sentences; nose-breathing-ish; you finish feeling you could do more. If in doubt, slow down. This is where aerobic base, capillaries, mitochondria, and injury-resilient tissue are built.
- **The 20% hard** is where you spend real effort: tempo, intervals, strides, race-pace work. It only "works" because the 80% easy lets you recover enough to hit it hard.

**Practical enforcement:** most runners need to slow their easy runs down. Use pace bands (below), heart rate (~< 75–80% max on easy), or the talk test. If they can't hold a conversation, it's not an easy run.

## 2. The training paces (Daniels' VDOT zones)
VDOT is a fitness score derived from a recent race time; it yields personalized paces. The five zones:

| Zone | Name | Effort / purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | Easy | Conversational, aerobic base, recovery | Most weekly volume, long runs |
| **M** | Marathon pace | Comfortably hard, race-specific endurance | Longer steady segments (HM/marathon prep) |
| **T** | Threshold / Tempo | "Comfortably hard," ~1-hr race effort, lactate threshold | 20–40 min tempo or cruise intervals |
| **I** | Interval | Hard, ~3–5 min reps at ~VO2max effort | 3–5 min reps w/ ~equal jog recovery |
| **R** | Repetition | Fast, short, for speed & economy | 200–400 m fast w/ full recovery |

**How to set paces without a lab:** plug a recent race (or a hard time trial — e.g. a 1-mile or 5k effort) into a VDOT calculator to get E/M/T/I/R paces. Re-test every ~6–8 weeks and update. For beginners with no race, set easy pace by the talk test and skip I/R at first.

## 3. Key workout types (the 20%)
- **Easy runs (E):** the base. Most days. Conversational.
- **Long run (E, some M):** the week's longest run at easy pace; builds durability and fat oxidation. Grow it gradually (see progression). For HM prep, work toward 16–18 km / 90–110 min; optionally sprinkle marathon-pace segments late.
- **Tempo / threshold (T):** sustained "comfortably hard" for **20–40 min** (or cruise intervals like 3–4 × 8–10 min at T with 1–2 min jog). Raises the pace you can hold before fatigue floods in — the single most valuable session for 5k–HM.
- **Intervals (I):** **3–5 min reps at ~VO2max effort** (e.g. 5–6 × 3 min hard / 2–3 min jog). Builds top-end aerobic power. Introduce only once a base is established.
- **Reps / strides (R):** short fast bursts — **strides** = 6–8 × 20 s relaxed-fast after an easy run; improve economy and turnover with low injury cost. Great, safe first "speed" for beginners.

**Beginners should master E + long run + strides first**, add tempo next, and only later add intervals. You don't need I/R work to run a solid first 5k or half.

## 4. Weekly structure templates

### Absolute beginner → first 5k (run/walk, Galloway-style)
- **3 runs/week**, all easy, using run/walk intervals that progress: e.g. wk1 = 8 × (1 min run / 90 s walk); progress to more run, less walk each week over ~8–10 weeks until continuous 5k.
- Add **1–2 easy Zone-2 cross-training** sessions (bike/row) if they want more without added impact.
- **1–2 strength sessions** (below). No tempo/intervals yet — just build the habit and the tissue.

### 5k / 10k improver (3–4 runs/week)
- Easy run · **Tempo** (20–30 min T) · Easy run + strides · **Long run** (easy). ~80/20 by time. Add short intervals (I) in the last few weeks before a 5k goal.

### Half-marathon build (4–5 runs/week, ~10–14 week block)
- **Mon:** rest or easy cross-train
- **Tue:** Quality — tempo (e.g. 2–4 × 10 min @ T) or intervals as the plan matures
- **Wed:** Easy + strides
- **Thu:** Easy (or second quality later in the block)
- **Fri:** rest / easy
- **Sat:** **Long run** (build toward 16–18 km / 90–110 min; add M-pace finishes late)
- **Sun:** Easy recovery run or off
- Peak weekly volume individualized; a common recreational HM peak is ~40–55 km/week, but scale to the runner's history. **Taper** the final ~1.5–2 weeks: cut volume ~30–50%, keep a little intensity, arrive fresh.

## 5. Progression & load management (injury avoidance — LFG specialty)
Running injuries are overwhelmingly **overuse from ramping too fast**, not bad luck. Manage load, not just mileage.

- **The "10% rule"** (don't raise weekly volume >10%) is a rough guardrail, not a law — the evidence behind it is weak. Use it as a soft cap, and lean on **acute:chronic workload** thinking instead: keep this week's load within roughly **0.8–1.3×** the average of the prior ~4 weeks (spikes above ~1.5 sharply raise injury risk).
- **Build in down weeks:** every ~3–4 weeks, cut volume ~20–30% to let tissue consolidate. Progress in a saw-tooth, not a straight line.
- **Progress ONE variable at a time:** add volume OR intensity in a given week, not both. Long-run distance and interval intensity shouldn't jump the same week.
- **Cadence:** if a runner overstrides / has high impact / recurrent shin or knee pain, a **~5–10% increase in cadence** (steps/min) reduces impact forces and overstriding — a simple, evidence-backed tweak. Don't force a number; nudge.
- **Surfaces & shoes:** vary surfaces where possible; replace worn shoes; avoid sudden switches to minimalist/max-drop shoes.
- **Strength train (see §6):** the best-supported "prehab" for runners.
- **Dubai heat:** May–Sept, default to early-morning outdoor or treadmill; heat inflates HR and effort, so run by effort not pace in the heat, hydrate and manage electrolytes.

### Common beginner injuries & first-line adjustments (see file 07 for detail + medical guardrails)
- **Shin splints (medial tibial stress syndrome, ~13–20% of runners):** almost always a load spike. Back off volume/impact, increase cadence slightly, add calf/tibialis strength, ensure gradual return. Persistent or focal bone pain → refer out (rule out stress fracture).
- **Runner's knee / IT band / Achilles / plantar issues:** reduce load, address the ramp, add hip/calf strength, return gradually. Sharp, worsening, or non-settling pain → medical referral.

**Golden rule:** pain that worsens during a run or lingers/increases day to day = back off and reassess; don't run through worsening pain.

## 6. Strength training for runners (the durability multiplier)
- **2× short strength sessions/week** measurably reduce injury risk and improve running economy. This is high-value, not optional, for LFG runners.
- **Emphasize:** heavy-ish, low-volume, compound lower-body (squat/hinge/single-leg — split squats, step-ups, calf raises, hip work) plus core. **2–4 sets, 4–8 reps, not to failure** — enough stimulus without wrecking the legs for running.
- **Placement:** lift after the hard run or on easy/quality-run days, keeping easy days genuinely easy. Avoid a leg-crushing lift the day before a long run or hard session.
- **Keep it minimal in peak weeks:** as running volume peaks, drop strength to a maintenance dose (1–2 short sessions) so it protects rather than competes.

---

## Running cheat sheet
- **80% easy, 20% hard.** If easy runs feel moderate, slow down.
- **Set paces from a recent race/time-trial via VDOT; re-test every 6–8 weeks.**
- **Beginner order:** run/walk → continuous easy → strides → tempo → intervals.
- **Half-marathon:** 4–5 runs/wk, one long run building to ~16–18 km, one tempo, rest easy; taper 1.5–2 weeks.
- **Injury avoidance:** keep weekly load ~0.8–1.3× the 4-week average, down-week every 3–4 weeks, progress one variable at a time, nudge cadence +5–10% if overstriding, and strength-train 2×/week.
- **Worsening/lingering pain → back off; bone pain or non-settling pain → refer out.**
