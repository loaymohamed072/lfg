## 1. IBS & common GI issues — low-FODMAP basics (Monash 3-phase)

IBS is common in our client base (bloating, cramps, irregular bowels). **Do not default IBS clients to "eat more fiber"** — it often makes them worse. The evidence-based tool is the **low-FODMAP diet (Monash University)**, run in **3 phases**. FODMAPs are fermentable carbs (Fermentable Oligo-, Di-, Mono-saccharides And Polyols) that draw water and ferment in the gut, triggering symptoms in sensitive people.

**Phase 1 — Elimination (2–6 weeks):** remove high-FODMAP foods; most responders improve within 2–6 weeks. *This is temporary and restrictive — not a forever diet.*
**Phase 2 — Reintroduction (6–8 weeks):** systematically reintroduce **one FODMAP subgroup at a time** against a low-FODMAP background to find personal triggers.
**Phase 3 — Personalization (ongoing):** eat freely, avoiding **only** the specific triggers found. Maximize variety.

**Common trigger groups (2024 RCT prevalence):** fructans (~56%) and mannitol (~54%) top the list, then GOS, lactose, fructose, sorbitol, glucose.

| Often high-FODMAP (screen) | Usually low-FODMAP swaps |
|---|---|
| Onion, garlic, wheat bread/pasta | Garlic-infused oil, chives, green tops of spring onion; sourdough spelt, oats, rice |
| Apples, pears, mango, watermelon, dried fruit | Banana (firm), berries, orange, grapes, kiwi |
| Milk, soft cheese, yogurt (lactose) | Lactose-free dairy, hard cheese, lactose-free yogurt |
| Beans, lentils, chickpeas (large servings) | Firm tofu, tempeh, small canned-and-rinsed legume portions |
| Cashews, pistachios, honey, high-fructose foods | Peanuts, macadamias, walnuts, maple syrup |

**LFG house approach for IBS:**
1. Screen and hit protein/calorie targets **using low-FODMAP protein sources** (meat, fish, eggs, firm tofu, lactose-free dairy, whey isolate is usually tolerated better than concentrate).
2. Suggest the 3-phase framework and **strongly recommend running it with an RD** (it's genuinely hard to do alone and over-restriction is a real risk).
3. Practical wins even without full FODMAP: smaller/regular meals, slow eating, adequate (not excessive) fiber ramped gradually, hydration, limit high-FODMAP + fried + very fatty foods, moderate caffeine/alcohol/carbonation, manage stress/sleep (gut-brain axis).
> **Refer out** if: severe/persistent symptoms, blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, or suspected IBD/celiac — that's a **doctor**, not a coach.

## 2. PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)

PCOS involves insulin resistance, higher androgens, irregular cycles, and difficulty losing weight. Nutrition helps meaningfully **through general good practice**, not a magic PCOS diet.

**What actually works (RD/guideline consensus):**
- **A modest calorie deficit + gradual fat loss** is the highest-impact lever — even ~5–10% bodyweight loss improves insulin sensitivity, cycles, and symptoms.
- **Higher protein** (helps satiety, muscle, glycemic control) — sit at the upper end, ~1.8–2.2 g/kg.
- **Prioritize fiber + lower-GI/whole-food carbs around training** to steady blood sugar; there's no need to go zero-carb — carb *quality and placement* beat carb elimination.
- **Resistance training + steps** improve insulin sensitivity independent of diet.
- No single "PCOS diet" wins; adherence + fat loss + protein + activity is the package.

> **Refer out** for: diagnosis, metformin/inositol/medication decisions, fertility, and any endocrine management → **doctor/RD**. The coach supports lifestyle; the clinician manages the condition.

## 3. Female physiology & the menstrual cycle

Coaching women well means accounting for the cycle instead of fighting it.

- **Scale swings with the cycle:** water retention rises in the **late luteal phase / pre-menstrual** week (can mask fat loss by 1–2+ kg of water). **Coach off 3–4-week trends**, and reassure clients a pre-period jump is water, not fat. This single point prevents a lot of quitting.
- **Cravings & hunger** often climb in the luteal phase — plan a little more food volume/protein/fiber, and don't label it a failure.
- **Keep the fat floor up** (≥0.5–1.0 g/kg, ≥20% kcal): chronically low fat + very low calories can disrupt cycles. **Loss of menstruation (amenorrhea) is a red flag** for under-fueling / RED-S → dial calories up and **refer to a doctor.**
- **Iron:** menstruating women lose iron; watch for fatigue/low ferritin, encourage iron-rich foods (red meat, legumes + vitamin C) — **testing/supplementing iron is a doctor's call.**
- Training/energy typically feels best in the follicular phase; some clients like to autoregulate hard sessions around the cycle. Optional, individual.

## 4. Ramadan & intermittent fasting

Ramadan is central for much of the LFG base: no food/water dawn→sunset for ~a month. Goal shifts to **maintain muscle, stay hydrated, and train sensibly** — usually a maintenance/recomp mindset, not aggressive dieting.

**Structure the two meals:**
- **Suhoor (pre-dawn):** the meal that protects the day. Slow carbs (oats, whole grains), **protein** (eggs, yogurt/labneh, cottage cheese), healthy fats (nuts, olive oil), fluids. **Under-eating protein at suhoor is the #1 cause of muscle loss in Ramadan** — anchor a solid protein serving here.
- **Iftar (sunset):** break with 2–3 dates + water, then a balanced meal — **protein** (chicken/fish/meat), complex carbs (rice, potato), veg. Avoid the fried-food + sweets overload that spikes then crashes energy.
- Distribute the day's protein across suhoor + iftar + a between-meals feeding to hit the daily target (aim to keep ~1.6–2.0 g/kg where possible).

**Hydration (critical in Dubai's heat):** target **~2.5–3 L of water across the non-fasting hours** (iftar→suhoor), include **electrolytes** (sodium, potassium, magnesium) to cut cramps/fatigue, and go easy on caffeine (diuretic) and very salty/sugary foods that worsen next-day thirst.

**Training in Ramadan:** best options — **after iftar** (most practical, refuel first), after Taraweeh, or before suhoor. Reduce **intensity/volume ~15–25%**, keep resistance training to hold muscle, add only light cardio. Don't chase PRs fasted in the heat.

**General intermittent fasting (non-Ramadan, e.g. 16:8):** it's simply a **meal-timing/adherence tool** — it works only if it creates a calorie deficit and the client likes it. No metabolic magic. Fine for clients who prefer fewer, larger meals; make sure protein target is still hit in the eating window.

## 5. Hot-climate hydration & electrolytes (Dubai default)

The UAE heat makes hydration a real performance and health issue, not a footnote (ACSM/NATA).

- **Baseline fluid:** ~30–40 ml/kg/day, **increased** for heat and training. Use urine color (pale straw = good) as a daily check.
- **Around training in heat:** aim to **lose no more than ~2% bodyweight** in a session; drink before, sip during, rehydrate after. A rough guide: ~500 ml in the 2–4 h pre-session, then replace sweat losses after (weigh before/after: each kg lost ≈ ~1.25–1.5 L to replace).
- **Electrolytes matter when sweating hard/long (>~1 h) or heavy/salty sweaters:** replace **sodium** (the big one), plus potassium/magnesium. Salt food normally, use an electrolyte drink for long/hot sessions. Plain water alone in high heat/high volume risks dilution.
- **Women** are somewhat more prone to exercise-associated **hyponatremia** (over-drinking plain water) — match fluid to sweat + include sodium, don't just chug water.
- Practical Dubai cues: hydrate through the day (not all at once), be extra deliberate in summer/outdoor training, and account for AC-driven low humidity increasing insensible losses.

## 6. Vegetarian & vegan clients

Fully workable for any goal with a little planning; the two watch-items are **protein sufficiency** and a few **micronutrients**.

- **Protein:** hit the same targets, leaning on tofu, tempeh, edamame, seitan (not for gluten-sensitive), legumes, soy milk, quinoa, and **plant/soy protein powder**. **Combine sources** across the day for a complete amino-acid profile; vegans may aim slightly higher (~1.6–2.2+ g/kg) to offset lower digestibility and leucine.
- **Micronutrients to flag (mostly a doctor/RD + supplement conversation):** **B12** (supplement — essential for vegans), iron (plant iron + vitamin C to boost absorption), **omega-3** (algae-based EPA/DHA), vitamin D, calcium, zinc, iodine.
- Fiber is naturally high → for **IBS-vegan** clients this collides with low-FODMAP; that's an RD case.
> **Refer out** vegan clients with fatigue, deficiency symptoms, or pregnancy to a **doctor/RD** for bloodwork and supplementation.

## 7. GLP-1 clients (Ozempic / Wegovy / Mounjaro / semaglutide / tirzepatide)

Increasingly common in a Dubai female base. These drugs suppress appetite hard, so the risk **inverts**: the danger is no longer overeating — it's **under-eating, low protein, and losing muscle** (GLP-1 weight loss can carry a high lean-mass share).

**Coach's job (lifestyle side only):**
- **Protect muscle:** push **protein to target despite low appetite** — practical guide ~**≥45 g per meal + 15–30 g per snack**, aiming ~1.8–2.0 g/kg; use easy high-protein forms (shakes, yogurt, eggs) when solid food is unappealing.
- **Resistance training is non-negotiable** — it's the main defense against muscle loss on these drugs. Run it like a **supervised recomp**.
- Manage common GI side-effects with smaller, protein-first, lower-fat/lower-fried meals and steady hydration + electrolytes.
- Watch for under-fueling, dizziness, and nutrient gaps.
> **Hard refer-out:** all **dosing, medication, and medical management stays with the prescriber.** The coach never advises starting, stopping, or changing a GLP-1. Coordinate; don't prescribe.

## 8. Menopause & perimenopause

Common in the female base and worth handling well. Falling estrogen tends to bring **muscle loss (sarcopenia risk), a lower metabolic rate, more central fat, and bone-density loss.**

**Highest-value nutrition/lifestyle levers (consensus):**
- **Higher protein** (≥1.6–2.2 g/kg) + **resistance training** to defend muscle and bone — the single most important combination.
- **Adequate calcium and vitamin D** for bone health (food first; supplementation is a doctor/RD call).
- A **modest** deficit if fat loss is the goal — aggressive cuts backfire (more muscle/bone loss, worse adherence, worse sleep).
- Support **sleep, stress, and protein-forward meals** to blunt cravings and preserve lean mass.
> **Refer out:** HRT and any medical management of menopause → **doctor.** The coach owns nutrition/training only.

## 9. Quick refer-out red-flag list (memorize)

Stop coaching and refer to a **doctor/RD** on any of: pregnancy/postpartum · suspected eating disorder or disordered-eating signs · loss of menstruation · blood in stool / unexplained weight loss / severe GI symptoms · type 1 or medicated diabetes · kidney/liver disease · IBD/celiac · deficiency symptoms · minors · anything requiring medication changes (including GLP-1 dosing). **When in doubt, refer out.** LFG's brand is safe, sustainable, evidence-based coaching — not clinical care.
