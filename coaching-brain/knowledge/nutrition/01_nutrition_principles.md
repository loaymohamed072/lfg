# 01 — Nutrition Principles (LFG Coaching Brain)

> **Sources synthesized:** Eric Helms *Muscle & Strength Pyramid: Nutrition* (hierarchy of priorities), ISSN Position Stands (protein, nutrient timing), Renaissance Periodization / *The Renaissance Diet 2.0* (Israetel et al.), Precision Nutrition (adherence & rate-of-change), Alan Aragon & Brad Schoenfeld (nutrient timing meta-analysis), Examine.com (evidence tiering), Layne Norton / Biolayne (energy balance & adaptation), Monash University & registered-dietitian consensus (fiber/GI). All explanations below are written original to LFG; frameworks are attributed, text is not reproduced.

This file is the **first thing the AI copilot reads before building any plan.** It sets the mental model. Everything downstream (calorie math, macros, meal templates, special cases) is an application of these principles.

---

## 1. The one law that never breaks: energy balance

Body fat changes are governed by the balance between energy in (food and drink) and energy out (resting metabolism + movement + digestion + exercise) over time.

- **Energy surplus** (in > out) → the body has spare energy → weight tends to rise (fat, and, if training + protein are present, muscle).
- **Energy deficit** (in < out) → the body must pull from stored energy → weight tends to fall (fat, and, if protein + training are low, some muscle).
- **Energy balance** (in ≈ out) → weight is roughly stable (this is "maintenance").

Two clarifications the copilot must hold:

1. **"Calories are the only thing that matters" is wrong, and "calories don't matter" is also wrong.** Calories decide the *direction and speed* of weight change. Macros, food choice, training, sleep, and adherence decide *what that weight is made of* and *whether the client can sustain it*. Both are true at once.
2. **The body is not a perfectly closed calculator.** Metabolic rate, hunger, and spontaneous movement (NEAT) shift in response to eating more or less. This is called **metabolic adaptation** (see §7). It slows things down; it does not break the law. Nobody escapes a real, sustained deficit.

**Coaching translation:** we never argue about whether energy balance is real. We argue about how to *create* the right balance in a way this specific client can live with.

## 2. The hierarchy of importance (Eric Helms' pyramid, LFG house version)

Attention should be spent in proportion to impact. Spending 80% of coaching effort on supplement timing while the client's calories are 700 over target is malpractice. The order, top = biggest lever:

| Priority | Lever | Rough share of the result | House rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Energy balance (calories)** | ~50% | Get this in the right ballpark first. Nothing below matters if this is wrong. |
| **2** | **Macronutrients** (protein first, then fat/carb split) | ~25% | Hit protein daily. Set fat floor and carb around training. |
| **3** | **Micronutrients, fiber, water** | ~15% | Mostly solved by whole foods, enough fruit/veg, fiber target, and drinking to thirst + climate. |
| **4** | **Nutrient / meal timing & distribution** | ~7% | Only matters at the margins. Distribute protein; fuel training. Avoid extremes, don't obsess. |
| **5** | **Supplements** | ~3% | Few work, and they work *little*. Never a fix for a broken level above. |

**How the copilot should use this:** when a client is stalled or asks a question, diagnose from the top down. Stalled fat loss? Check adherence to calories *before* discussing carb timing. Poor recovery? Check total protein and sleep *before* recommending a supplement. Always fix the highest broken level first.

## 3. Protein — the single most important macro to get right

Protein does the most work per gram for a physique/performance client:

- **Preserves (or builds) lean muscle**, especially in a deficit. This is the difference between "losing weight" and "losing fat and looking better."
- **Most satiating macro** — keeps clients fuller per calorie, which directly helps adherence.
- **Highest thermic effect of food** (~20–30% of protein calories are burned digesting it, vs ~5–10% carbs, ~0–3% fat), a small but real free advantage.

**Daily targets (LFG house numbers, from ISSN position stand + Helms + RP):**

| Situation | Protein target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General health / sedentary | 1.2–1.6 g/kg bodyweight | Above the RDA (0.8 g/kg), which is a floor for deficiency, not an optimum. |
| Building muscle, training hard | 1.6–2.2 g/kg | ISSN sweet spot. ~1.6 captures most of the benefit; go higher for insurance. |
| **Fat loss while lifting (most LFG clients)** | **1.8–2.4 g/kg** (or ~2.0 g/kg of a realistic goal weight for higher-body-fat clients) | Higher protein in a deficit protects muscle and controls hunger. |
| Very lean, aggressive cut, physique prep | up to 2.4–3.0 g/kg | Diminishing returns, but useful insurance when calories are very low. |

- **Practical shortcut for clients who think in pounds:** ~0.7–1.0 g per pound of bodyweight covers almost everyone.
- **Higher-body-fat clients:** base protein on **goal/lean bodyweight**, not total scale weight, so you don't massively over-shoot (a 120 kg client at 40% fat does not need 264 g).
- **Distribution:** roughly even across 3–5 meals, ~0.4 g/kg (≈20–40 g) per meal, spaced ~3–4 h, to keep muscle-protein-synthesis topped up (ISSN). This is a **refinement**, not a make-or-break; total daily protein matters most.

## 4. Fat and carbohydrate — set a fat floor, flex the rest

After calories and protein are locked, fat and carbs split the remaining energy. Neither is "bad." Both have floors.

- **Dietary fat floor:** keep fat at **~0.5–1.0 g/kg bodyweight**, and generally not below **~20% of total calories**, to protect hormones (including menstrual and thyroid health), vitamin absorption, and satiety. RP's rule of thumb: fat should not drop below roughly the minimum needed for hormonal health even on the leanest cut.
- **Carbohydrate is the primary training fuel.** It powers hard resistance training and higher-intensity work, spares protein, and aids recovery and sleep. Clients who train hard generally *feel and perform better* with meaningful carbs. Carbs are the main lever we *flex up around training and down on rest days*.
- **Low-carb is a tool, not a rule.** It works for fat loss only because it usually cuts calories and boosts satiety, not through magic. Some clients prefer it; performance in the gym often suffers. House position: **carbs are neutral-to-helpful for training clients; only go low-carb if the client prefers it and it improves their adherence.**

**Priority within macros:** Protein (fixed target) > Fat (floor) > Carbs (fill the rest, concentrate around training).

## 5. Fiber — the quiet lever for satiety, gut health, and adherence

Fiber punches above its weight for a coaching population:

- Increases **fullness** per calorie (slows gastric emptying, adds food volume) → easier deficit.
- Feeds gut bacteria, supports **regular digestion** (relevant to our IBS clients — but see the special-cases file, because for IBS the *type* of fiber matters and blanket "eat more fiber" can backfire).
- Improves **glycemic control** and supports cholesterol/heart health.

**Target:** ~**14 g per 1000 kcal**, or roughly **25 g/day for women, 30–38 g/day for men** (registered-dietitian consensus). Come from vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, oats, seeds.

**House caveat:** ramp fiber up *gradually* with water, or clients get bloating and gas. For IBS clients, do NOT default to a high-fiber push — route to the special-cases file.

## 6. Hydration, satiety, and food quality — the "level 3" cluster

**Hydration.** Water supports performance, digestion, appetite regulation (thirst is often mis-read as hunger), and recovery. Baseline: **~30–40 ml/kg/day** (≈2–3 L for most adults) as a starting anchor, adjusted **up** for training and hot climate. In Dubai's heat this is not optional — see the hydration/electrolyte section in the special-cases file. Drinking to thirst plus a visible pale-yellow-urine check works for most; heavy sweaters and hot-climate clients need sodium too.

**Satiety — engineer it, don't rely on willpower.** Adherence is mostly a satiety problem. Build meals from foods that are filling per calorie:

- **High-satiety builders:** lean protein, high-volume vegetables, fruit, potatoes, oats, legumes, broth-based soups, Greek yogurt.
- **Easy-to-overeat (calorie-dense, low-satiety):** oils, nuts/nut butters, cheese, dried fruit, refined baked goods, liquid calories (juice, soda, fancy coffees), alcohol. Not banned — *budgeted*.
- The lever: **protein + fiber + water volume + minimally processed foods** = fuller on fewer calories.

**Food quality.** ~80–90% minimally processed, nutrient-dense whole foods; ~10–20% "whatever fits and keeps you sane." This ratio hits micronutrients, fiber, and satiety automatically while leaving room for a life. Perfectly "clean" eating that the client abandons in three weeks loses to slightly-imperfect eating they hold for a year.

## 7. Metabolic adaptation (the honest version) — *house line*

**Controversy flag:** "starvation mode" and "metabolic damage" are mostly myth as usually described. What is real is **metabolic adaptation / adaptive thermogenesis.**

- When you diet, TDEE falls **more than bodyweight loss alone predicts**: resting metabolism dips slightly, NEAT (fidgeting, spontaneous movement) drops, hunger rises, and you unconsciously move less. The larger and longer the deficit, the more adaptation (Layne Norton / Biolayne; MATADOR-type data).
- This **slows** fat loss and makes maintenance harder. It does **not** stop a real deficit and does **not** cause fat gain on a genuine deficit.
- **The fix is not eating less.** It's smaller sensible deficits, adequate protein, resistance training to defend muscle, managing stress/sleep, and structured breaks at maintenance (see the diet-breaks/reverse-dieting section of the fat-loss file).

**House recommendation for the copilot:** never tell a client they've "damaged their metabolism." Explain adaptation honestly, then act on it with a moderate deficit, high protein, lifting, and periodic maintenance phases.

## 8. Adherence over perfection — the meta-principle

The best diet is the one the client actually follows. Every LFG plan is scored on **can-they-sustain-this**, not on textbook optimality.

Operating rules the copilot builds every plan around:

1. **Fit the plan to the client, not the client to the plan** — their food preferences, schedule, culture (halal, regional foods), budget, cooking skill, and social life are inputs, not obstacles.
2. **Flexible over rigid.** Flexible dieting / "IIFYM within a whole-food base" beats rigid meal plans on long-term adherence for most people. Keep the framework, let the foods flex.
3. **Minimum effective dose.** Use the smallest deficit/surplus and the fewest rules that still produce progress. Save the aggressive tools for when they're needed.
4. **Consistency beats intensity.** 85% adherence for a year outperforms 100% for a month followed by a rebound.
5. **Plan for real life.** Eating out, travel, Ramadan, social events, and cravings are expected and built in, not treated as failures.
6. **Progress is measured over weeks, not days.** Daily weight is noise (water, sodium, cycle, glycogen, gut contents). Trends over 2–4 weeks are signal.

**Bottom line for the AI:** lead with energy balance and protein, engineer satiety and adherence, respect the client's real constraints, and only reach for advanced levers (timing, refeeds, supplements) once the fundamentals are locked. That order is the entire game.
