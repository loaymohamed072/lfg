# 06 — Supplements (LFG Coaching Brain)

> **Sources synthesized:** Examine.com (evidence tiering & dosing), ISSN Position Stands (protein, creatine, caffeine, nutrient timing), Eric Helms *Muscle & Strength Pyramid* (supplements as the smallest lever), registered-dietitian consensus, Renaissance Periodization. Original explanations; evidence-tier labels are Examine-style (Strong / Moderate / Limited / No good evidence).

**Frame first (from the Principles file):** supplements are **level 5 — the smallest lever.** Very few work, the ones that work help *a little*, and none fix a broken diet, training, or sleep. This file's job is to keep the copilot **recommending only what's worth money and skipping the rest.** In the UAE, also prefer **third-party-tested** products (NSF Certified for Sport / Informed Sport) for quality and to avoid banned contaminants.

---

## Evidence tiers used below
- 🟢 **Strong** — consistent high-quality RCT evidence; worth recommending when relevant.
- 🟡 **Moderate** — helps some people / some contexts; conditional.
- 🟠 **Limited** — weak or mixed evidence; only case-by-case.
- 🔴 **Skip** — no good evidence, or benefit not worth the cost/risk.

---

## 1. The short list that actually works 🟢

### Creatine monohydrate — 🟢 the best-evidenced sports supplement
- **What it does:** improves strength, power, high-intensity training capacity, and lean mass; may aid recovery and cognition. Works for **men and women**.
- **Dose:** **3–5 g/day, every day, indefinitely.** Timing doesn't matter — take it whenever is consistent. Optional loading (~20 g/day split into 4 for 5–7 days) saturates faster but isn't necessary; steady 3–5 g gets there in ~3–4 weeks.
- **Form:** **monohydrate only.** Fancy forms (HCL, buffered, etc.) are costlier with no advantage. Buy plain, tested monohydrate.
- **Notes:** the ~1–2 kg early scale bump is **water inside muscle**, not fat — pre-warn clients (especially women worried about the scale). Safe for healthy people long-term. Well-tolerated; take with food if any minor GI upset.

### Whey / protein powder — 🟢 (a convenient food, not a magic pill)
- **What it does:** an easy, high-quality way to **hit the daily protein target** — that's the entire benefit. If a client already hits protein from food, they don't need it.
- **Dose:** ~20–40 g per serving as needed to reach the daily total; **whey isolate** if lactose-sensitive; **soy/pea/blend** for vegans.
- **Notes:** this is our #1 practical recommendation because protein adherence is the most common gap. Bars/RTDs count too — just watch total calories.

### Caffeine — 🟢 reliable performance & focus aid
- **What it does:** improves strength, power, endurance, focus, and perceived effort; blunts appetite slightly.
- **Dose:** **3–6 mg/kg bodyweight, ~30–60 min pre-training.** No extra benefit above ~9 mg/kg — more just brings jitters/insomnia. (E.g., a 68 kg client: ~200–400 mg.)
- **Notes:** mind total daily intake and afternoon timing (protect sleep — sleep beats any supplement). Ramadan: shift to the eating window and watch the diuretic effect. Tolerance builds; cycle if needed.

### Vitamin D3 — 🟡→🟢 (context-dependent; likely relevant here)
- **What it does:** supports bone, immune, muscle, and hormonal health. Benefits are clearest when **correcting a deficiency**, which is common — including in the Gulf, where heavy sun-avoidance, indoor life, and covering can leave people low despite the climate.
- **Dose:** commonly **~1000–2000 IU/day** as general maintenance; deficiency-correction dosing should be **guided by bloodwork and a doctor.**
- **Notes:** test if suspected low; take with a fatty meal (fat-soluble). Don't megadose blindly.

### Omega-3 (EPA/DHA fish or algae oil) — 🟡 general health
- **What it does:** supports cardiovascular health, modulates inflammation, supports brain/mood; small, inconsistent direct effects on body comp/recovery.
- **Dose:** **~1–3 g/day combined EPA+DHA.** Useful mainly for clients eating little oily fish; **algae-based** for vegans.
- **Notes:** buy a quality/tested product (oxidation matters). A "nice general-health add," not a physique changer.

### Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) — 🟡 situational, higher value in Dubai
- **What it does:** replace sweat losses; reduce cramps/fatigue; support hydration in heat and long/intense sessions.
- **Use:** long/hot training (>~1 h), heavy/salty sweaters, Ramadan rehydration windows, low-carb clients (who shed water/sodium). Sodium is the main one.
- **Notes:** most clients get enough from salting food normally; reach for electrolyte products for genuine heat/volume demands (see hydration section in file 05).

## 2. Conditional / case-by-case 🟡–🟠

- **Magnesium — 🟡** may help sleep and cramps *if* intake is low; glycinate form for sleep/GI comfort. Not a universal need.
- **Multivitamin — 🟡** cheap insurance for clients with restrictive diets, poor variety, or dieting hard on low calories; not a substitute for food. Doesn't build muscle or burn fat.
- **Fiber supplement (psyllium) — 🟡** useful when whole-food fiber is hard to hit, or for some constipation; **caution in IBS** (can help or hurt — RD territory).
- **Probiotics — 🟠** strain-specific and mixed; may help certain IBS clients — individual trial, RD-guided.
- **Beta-alanine — 🟡** small endurance benefit for repeated high-intensity efforts in the ~1–4 min range; ~3–6 g/day (split to reduce tingling). Niche — only for the right training.
- **Citrulline malate — 🟠** modest pump/volume and possible small performance/soreness benefit; ~6–8 g pre-workout. Minor.
- **Collagen + vitamin C — 🟠** emerging, modest support for tendon/joint/connective tissue (does **not** count toward your muscle-building protein target). Optional for joint-niggle clients.
- **Ashwagandha — 🟠** limited evidence for stress/sleep/recovery; individual. Not a physique tool.
- **Iron / B12 — 🟡 but clinical:** relevant for menstruating women, vegans, and fatigue — **only supplement on bloodwork + doctor guidance** (iron can be harmful if not needed).

---

## 3. Skip these (waste of money, or the benefit isn't there) 🔴

- **Fat burners / "thermogenics"** — mostly caffeine + fillers; no meaningful fat loss beyond the caffeine. The deficit burns the fat, not the pill.
- **BCAAs / EAAs** *for anyone eating enough protein* — redundant; whole protein already contains them. (EAAs only have a narrow case for very-low-protein or fasted-training edge cases.)
- **Testosterone boosters / "test support"** — don't meaningfully raise testosterone in healthy people.
- **CLA, raspberry ketones, garcinia, "detox teas," carb blockers, waist-trainer wraps** — no credible fat-loss evidence.
- **Glutamine** (for healthy trainees) — no performance/immune benefit at normal intakes.
- **Most pre-workout "proprietary blends"** — you're usually paying for caffeine you could buy cheaply and cleanly; the rest is under-dosed. If a client wants a pre-workout, it's really caffeine (± creatine, ± citrulline) they're after.

## 4. LFG default supplement stack (what the copilot actually recommends)

For a **typical training client, food and training already dialed in**, the whole worthwhile stack is short and cheap:

| Priority | Supplement | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Creatine monohydrate** | 3–5 g/day | Best-evidenced, cheap, works for everyone |
| 2 | **Protein powder** | as needed to hit target | Solves the #1 adherence gap |
| 3 | **Caffeine** | 3–6 mg/kg pre-training | Reliable performance/focus |
| 4 | **Vitamin D3** | ~1000–2000 IU (test if low) | Common deficiency, esp. Gulf indoor lifestyles |
| 5 | **Omega-3** | 1–3 g EPA+DHA | General health, low-fish diets |
| Situational | **Electrolytes** | per session/heat | Dubai heat, long/hot training, Ramadan |

Everything else is optional and case-by-case. **Two hard rules for the copilot:**
1. **Never let a supplement recommendation substitute for fixing calories, protein, training, or sleep.** If those aren't in place, that's the conversation — not the shelf.
2. **In the UAE, prefer third-party-tested brands** (NSF Certified for Sport / Informed Sport) for quality and contamination safety, and flag that **anyone with a medical condition, pregnant, or on medication should clear supplements with a doctor first.**
