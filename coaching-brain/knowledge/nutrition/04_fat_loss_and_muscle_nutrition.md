# 04 — Fat Loss & Muscle Nutrition (LFG Coaching Brain)

> **Sources synthesized:** Murphy & Koehler 2021 meta-analysis (deficit size vs lean mass), MATADOR study (Byrne et al., intermittent energy restriction) + 2024 pooled meta-analyses on diet breaks, Eric Helms *Muscle & Strength Pyramid*, Renaissance Periodization / *Renaissance Diet 2.0*, Precision Nutrition (rate-of-change), Layne Norton / Biolayne (metabolic adaptation & reverse dieting), ISSN (protein for muscle). Original explanations; each controversial topic carries an explicit **house recommendation**.

This file is the **protocol library** for the four goals LFG runs: fat loss, muscle gain, recomp, and the phase-management tools (breaks, refeeds, reverse diet, plateaus). Everything here sits on top of the calorie/macro setup in file 02.

## 1. Fat-loss protocol (the LFG default engine)

**Goal:** maximize *fat* loss while defending muscle and adherence. The whole game is a *moderate* deficit + *high* protein + *lifting*.

**Setup (from file 02):**
- Calories: **−15–20% below TDEE** (moderate default); up to −25% for higher-body-fat/urgent, well-adhering clients.
- Rate: **0.5–1.0% bodyweight/week.** Faster ≠ better — Murphy & Koehler show lean-mass loss climbs with deficit size; >1.5%/wk roughly doubles the muscle share.
- **Protein high: 1.8–2.4 g/kg** (this is non-negotiable in a deficit — it's the primary muscle-protection lever).
- **Resistance training** to give the body a reason to keep muscle. Cardio/steps for extra deficit, not as the main tool.
- Fat ≥ floor (0.5–1.0 g/kg); carbs fill the rest, biased around training.

**Running it:**
1. Weigh daily, judge the **weekly average trend** over 2–3 weeks.
2. On target → don't touch it (the most common coaching error is changing a working plan).
3. Stalled after 2–3 wk of real adherence → cut ~100–200 kcal (carbs/fat) **or** add ~2000 steps/day (prefer activity).
4. Protect sleep and manage stress — both blunt fat loss and spike hunger/adaptation.
5. Expect **non-linear** loss: whooshes and stalls from water, sodium, cycle, glycogen, gut contents. Trend > daily.

**When to end a fat-loss phase:** goal reached, adherence eroding, hunger/fatigue/strength persistently down, or 3+ weeks stalled at a sensible deficit → transition to **maintenance / diet break / reverse** (§5–7), don't just grind lower.

## 2. Muscle-gain nutrition (lean bulk)

**Goal:** maximize muscle, minimize fat gain. Patience is the entire skill.

- Calories: **+5–15% above TDEE** (≈ +200–350 kcal/day). Beginners: low end. The leaner/more advanced, the slower.
- Rate: **~0.25–0.5% bodyweight/week** (intermediates ~1–2 lb/month; true beginners can push ~0.5%/wk). Faster than that is fat, not muscle — you can't force muscle to grow faster than physiology allows.
- **Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg** (~1 g/lb is the classic, and it's plenty).
- **Carbs are the priority fuel** — drive them up to power progressive-overload training and recovery; concentrate around workouts.
- Progress check: scale + gym performance every 2–3 wk. Gaining too fast/soft → trim surplus. Flat for 2–3 wk → add ~100–150 kcal (carbs).
- **Bulk length:** run a gaining phase for months, not weeks; muscle accrues slowly. Females and older clients gain more slowly — set expectations honestly.

## 3. Body recomposition (lose fat + gain muscle simultaneously)

Real, but only in specific windows. Best candidates: **beginners, returning/detrained lifters, higher-body-fat clients, anyone on a GLP-1, and those returning from a layoff.** Advanced lean lifters recomp very slowly and are usually better off alternating cut/gain phases.

**Recomp setup:**
- Calories: **maintenance to ~10% below** (−100 to −250 kcal/day) — small or zero deficit.
- **Protein high: 2.0–2.4 g/kg** (the key that lets muscle build while fat drops).
- **Progressive resistance training** is mandatory — it's the signal that partitions energy toward muscle.
- Expect a **stable or slowly-moving scale** while the mirror, tape, and lifts improve. This is the phase where the copilot must **coach off photos, measurements, and strength**, not the scale, or the client quits thinking "nothing's happening."

---

## 4. Realistic rate-of-change targets (set expectations up front)

| Metric | Realistic range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fat loss** | 0.5–1.0% BW/week | Leaner clients & women trend to the slower end |
| **Muscle gain (men)** | ~0.25–0.5 kg/month (beginner), less for intermediates | Advanced: ~1–2 kg/**year** |
| **Muscle gain (women)** | roughly **half** the male rate | Physiology, not effort |
| **Recomp** | slow on both; weeks-to-months to see clearly | Judge by photos/tape/lifts |
| Beginner "newbie gains" | fastest fat loss + muscle gain window | Prioritize recomp here |

The copilot's job is to **cap the client's expectations to reality** before they set them too high and quit when the fantasy doesn't arrive.

## 5. Diet breaks — *house-recommended tool*

A **diet break** = a planned return to **maintenance calories for ~1–2 weeks** in the middle of a longer fat-loss phase (keep protein high, raise carbs/fat back to maintenance).

**Why we use them:**
- **Psychological reset** — biggest real benefit; relieves diet fatigue and improves long-run adherence.
- **May partially reverse metabolic adaptation** and normalize hunger/hormone signals.
- **The evidence is honest here:** the MATADOR study showed intermittent dieters lost *more* fat and kept it off better — **but** under extreme conditions (33% deficit, all food provided). When 2024 meta-analyses pooled all 12 trials, the *fat-loss advantage of diet breaks disappeared.*

**House recommendation:** use diet breaks **for adherence and sanity, not because they burn more fat.** Program one every **~6–12 weeks** of dieting, or when hunger/fatigue/adherence is clearly slipping. They make long diets survivable — that's the win.

---

## 6. Refeeds — *situational tool*

A **refeed** = a shorter planned bump (1–2 days) to **maintenance-ish via extra carbs** (keep protein, keep fat modest), inside an ongoing deficit.

- Useful for: refilling muscle glycogen before a hard training block, a mental/food break, and topping up leptin/hormones on lean clients.
- Best for **leaner, hard-training** clients; less impactful for higher-body-fat clients (who have plenty of stored energy already).
- **House position:** a nice-to-have, not required. Diet breaks (§5) do more for the average LFG client than scattered refeeds. Use refeeds mainly for lean/athletic clients or around demanding training.

## 7. Reverse dieting — *honest, deflated house line*

**Reverse dieting** = gradually adding calories (commonly +50–150 kcal/week, mostly carbs) after a diet, aiming to return to maintenance while minimizing fat regain.

**The honest evidence (Layne Norton's own evolved view; Alan Aragon; Biolayne):**
- The old story — that slow reversing "repairs the metabolism" and lets you eat far more at the same leanness — is **overstated.** "Metabolic damage" is a myth; **metabolic adaptation is real but modest and largely reverses when you eat more and gain a little weight back** regardless of how carefully you do it.
- The genuine value of reversing is **behavioral and control-based:** it's a structured, low-anxiety way to exit a diet, keep tracking, avoid a binge rebound, and let hunger/hormones normalize *gradually* instead of overshooting.

**House recommendation:** offer reverse dieting as a **controlled exit ramp from a fat-loss phase for clients prone to post-diet bingeing** — add ~5–10% calories/week back toward maintenance, keep protein high, monitor weight. Do **not** sell it as a metabolic-repair miracle. For a stable, disciplined client, simply stepping back up to maintenance is perfectly fine.

## 8. Plateaus — diagnose top-down before touching the plan

A true plateau = **3+ weeks of no trend change** with genuinely good adherence. Most "plateaus" are actually adherence, tracking drift, or water-masking. Diagnose in order:

1. **Adherence & tracking accuracy** — untracked bites, oil, sauces, weekend blowouts, liquid calories. #1 cause. Re-audit the log honestly.
2. **Water masking** — up sodium/carbs, cycle phase, new training, stress/cortisol, poor sleep can hide fat loss on the scale for weeks. Check tape/photos, not just weight.
3. **Adaptation & lower NEAT** — TDEE has fallen (less spontaneous movement + lighter body). Add steps before cutting calories.
4. **Genuine plateau (only after 1–3 ruled out):** either (a) small calorie cut (~100–200 kcal), (b) add activity/steps, or (c) run a **diet break at maintenance** to reset adherence and adaptation, then resume.

**House order of operations:** *check adherence → check water/stress/sleep → add steps → then cut calories → and use a diet break when the client is fried.* Never crash calories in response to a plateau.

## 9. Female & GLP-1 quick pointers (full detail in file 05)

- **Female clients:** slower muscle gain and fat loss than males; scale swings hard with the menstrual cycle (water pre-period) — coach off multi-week trends; keep the fat floor up for hormonal health; strength + high protein protect against under-eating.
- **GLP-1 (Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro) clients:** appetite is suppressed, so the risk flips from *overeating* to *muscle loss from under-eating and low protein.* Prioritize hitting **protein (≥1.8–2.0 g/kg, ~45 g/meal + 15–30 g/snack)** and **resistance training**; effectively run a supervised **recomp**. This is a medical context — coordinate with their prescriber (see file 05).
