# Flexible Dieting — Coaching Distillation

**Source:** Alan Aragon, *Flexible Dieting* (Victory Belt, 2022). Original paraphrase for coaching use; no verbatim text reproduced. Aragon is lead author of the ISSN Position Stand on Diets & Body Composition, so these numbers track the current evidence-based consensus.

This is the applied core a coach can use to set up and defend a diet. Every number here is a starting range to adjust off the client's response, not a rule. Aragon's whole thesis: the diet that works is the one the client actually follows, so build for adherence first and precision second.

---

## 1. The flexible-dieting stance (and what it is NOT)

Aragon separates **flexible dieting** from the IIFYM caricature. His distinctions:

- **Flexible vs rigid dietary control** has been studied since the early 1990s. Flexible control tracks with better long-term weight regulation; rigid, all-or-nothing control tracks with disordered eating, binge behavior, and failure. Rigid dieting runs on dichotomous thinking, foods become "good" vs "bad," which loads the dieter with guilt and eventually breaks adherence.
- **Flexible dieting is not a specific diet, and it is not synonymous with IIFYM or "counting macros."** IIFYM began as a bodybuilding-forum joke and got twisted into "eat all the junk you want if it fits your macros." Aragon rejects that reading.
- Flexible dieting means individualizing the four levers: **macro targets, food choices, meal timing, and the tracking/accountability method** — and, the hidden gem, the *degree of flexibility itself*. A client who does better with more structure gets more structure. Hybrids of rigid and flexible control are fair game.

**The load-bearing heuristic — discretionary calorie allowance:** 80–90% of intake from whole and minimally refined foods; **10–20% discretionary** for any food the client wants (added fats, added sugar, alcohol, indulgence foods). On a 2,000 kcal diet that is 200–400 kcal/day of "whatever you want." This is how quality and enjoyment coexist. A 100%-clean diet is neither required nor superior for adherence.

## 2. Food quality vs quantity

Quantity (total calories) drives body composition; quality drives health and, indirectly, adherence. Aragon does not moralize food. His position on quality:
- **Fiber:** minimum **25–29 g/day (~14 g per 1,000 kcal)** for lower all-cause and CVD mortality. Fiber is not truly calorie-free (~2 kcal/g), but higher fiber intake independently promotes greater weight loss and adherence.
- **Glycemic index / glycemic load: irrelevant distractions** once you get total macros right, minimize highly refined foods, and hit adequate fiber. Don't coach GI/GL.
- **Resistant starch** (e.g. cooked-then-cooled rice) does not meaningfully increase satiety or reduce intake. Skip the hype.
- Prefers the term **"indulgence foods"** over "junk," and they don't have to be forced in if the client doesn't want them.

## 3. Protein — his priority macro

**Intake hierarchy of importance:** (1) total daily amount, (2) distribution across the day, (3) timing relative to training. Total dwarfs the rest.

Total daily targets (per kg bodyweight unless noted):
- General population / general health: **1.2–1.6 g/kg**
- Maximize muscle growth: **1.6–2.2 g/kg (0.7–1 g/lb)**
- Athletes in a deficit (hypocaloric): **1.6–2.4 g/kg**
- Maximize athletic performance (position-stand range): **1.4–2.0 g/kg**
- Lean, resistance-trained, dieting hard, "pushing the envelope": **2.2–3.3 g/kg of target bodyweight**, or **2.3–3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass**
- Recomposition seen at **2.6–3.5 g/kg FFM**
- FFM-based simple baseline: **2 g/kg FFM (~0.9 g/lb FFM)**; muscle-gain or dieting clients start **2.2 g/kg FFM (1 g/lb FFM)**

Practical simplification: base protein on **goal/target bodyweight** (or current bodyweight if maintaining) to approximate lean mass with a built-in safety margin, instead of fussing over FFM. Leaner and more aggressively dieting = push protein higher, because protein is the layer that protects lean mass under a deficit.

**Distribution & timing (only worth attention for maximizing muscle):**
- Minimum **4 feedings/day at ~0.4–0.55 g/kg per meal**, spread evenly waking-to-pre-bed. Three feedings is likely fine outside competitive contexts.
- Per-meal minimum to overcome anabolic resistance: **25–30 g** (older adults, ~2.5–2.8 g leucine); Aragon treats that as a floor, not a target.
- **No narrow post-workout "anabolic window."** The anabolic effect of a protein meal lasts **~3–5 hours**. Focus on the **periworkout period**: a protein dose of **0.4–0.55 g/kg (~30–50 g)** within ~2 hours before and again within ~2 hours after training. Intra-workout protein only matters if you trained fasted or the session exceeds ~2 hours.
- **Plant vs animal:** animal protein stimulates MPS more efficiently, but a vegan diet at **1.6 g/kg total** produced comparable size/strength gains (in untrained subjects). Total protein wins.

## 4. Carbohydrate

- 4 kcal/g. **Not an essential nutrient** for survival, but arguably essential for maximizing high-intensity performance and muscle. Ketogenic-level restriction (<10% kcal, ~≤50 g) compromises lean-mass gain — a compromise, not a deal-breaker.
- Hypertrophy: **3–8 g/kg/day**. Strength/power: **5–8 g/kg/day** (safe minimum for strength ~3 g/kg). General fitness, no performance goal: **~3–5 g/kg** (and a minimum is arguably unnecessary). Endurance: **6–12 g/kg**.
- No sex-based difference in requirements.
- Added sugar recommendations across health bodies are inconsistent (5–25% of kcal) and weakly evidenced — just fold added sugar into the 10–20% discretionary allowance.

## 5. Fat

- 9 kcal/g, **essential** (EFA needs). Minimum to avoid deficiency ~10% of kcal, but that floor is not the same as an optimal, sustainable intake.
- Recommended total range: **0.7–2.2 g/kg (0.32–1.0 g/lb)**.
- **Saturated fat:** most bodies say keep below 10% of kcal, but Aragon flags real nuance — lean unprocessed red meat fits healthy patterns in controlled trials; up to **1 egg/day** shows no CVD signal (several studies fine at 3/day). Industrially produced trans fats are the real villain; ruminant trans fats (dairy/meat) are largely benign.
- No sex-specific fat targets. Pre-exercise "fat loading" fails when tested and can impair high-intensity output.

## 6. Alcohol

Aragon does not ban it — he files it under **discretionary calories**. Technically **7 kcal/g**, debatable as a true macronutrient. He notes it is **ergolytic** (performance-impairing). Coaching read: alcohol is allowed inside the 10–20% discretionary budget; the honest costs are the calories, impaired recovery/performance, and appetite disinhibition, not moral failure.

## 7. Meal frequency

His own 2015 meta-analysis (15 studies): **meal frequency did not change total bodyweight**; higher frequency associated with slightly more fat loss and lean retention, but that effect vanished when a single study was removed. **Verdict: meal number comes down to personal preference for adherence.** Timing/distribution matter only for the muscle-maximizing edge case (and mainly at very low frequency of 1–2 meals/day).

## 8. Supplements — what he rates and debunks

**Debunks / low value:**
- **Fat-loss supplements:** poor risk-to-benefit across the board, some dangerous (stimulant stacks). "The best weight-loss supplement is a caloric deficit."
- **Caffeine for fat loss:** does reduce weight/fat dose-dependently, but he would *not* use it for that — other levers offer more benefit without the risk.

**The "heavyweights" he endorses** (evidence-backed performance supplements):
- **Creatine monohydrate** — best-studied, cheapest, safest form. Maintenance **3–5 g/day** (0.04–0.07 g/kg); optional load **20–25 g/day for 5–7 days** (1–2 kg lean gain possible, partly water). No load needed — saturation hits in ~4 weeks anyway. Safe long-term (30 g/day for 5 years showed no harm). Works fine with coffee/tea.
- **Beta-alanine** — raises muscle carnosine, buffers acidosis, helps high-intensity endurance (mid strength-endurance continuum).
- **Sodium bicarbonate** (baking soda) — buffers acidosis; benefits 30 s to 12 min efforts. Dose **0.2–0.5 g/kg** (>0.4 g/kg raises GI-upset risk), ~90 min pre-exercise; chronic dosing reduces GI distress.
- **Nitrate** (beetroot) — vasodilation; benefits ~12–40 min efforts (endurance capacity more than time trials).
- **Caffeine** — for performance: **3–6 mg/kg, 60 min pre-exercise**; ~2 mg/kg may be the minimum effective; 9 mg/kg is high-risk with no added benefit.
- **Multivitamin-mineral (MVM):** pragmatically warranted despite null disease-prevention research, especially for active people in hypocaloric phases or eating a limited food variety. "Lift heavy and take a multi" is dumb-sounding but not bad advice.

## 9. How he sets up a diet

Two frameworks, differing only by macro order:
- **APS (Athletic Performance Secondary — most clients):** 1) goal + time frame → 2) calories → 3) protein → 4) fat → 5) fill remainder with carbs.
- **APP (Athletic Performance Primary — competitive athletes):** same, but set **carbs before fat** (steps 4 and 5 swap), because performance depends on carb availability.

**Calories — the Aragon Formula** (uses pounds; target bodyweight = "ideal" bodyweight):
1. `TBW × (10 + total weekly training hours)`
2. Multiply by NEAT factor: **1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.4** (low / moderate / high / very high non-exercise activity).
3. "Fudge factor": if the client tends to under-report while dieting, subtract 10%; if they under-eat while gaining, add 10%; otherwise skip.

Formulas are ballpark starting points, not gospel — a client who knows their historical intake may not need one. Traditional alternative: estimate maintenance, then apply a **~250–500 kcal** surplus or deficit.

**Rate-of-change guidelines:**
- Fat loss preserving lean mass: **1–2 lb/week (~0.5–1% bodyweight/week)**; fatter clients can go faster.
- Muscle gain (beginner/intermediate): **1–2 lb/month**; women similar proportionally, ~30% less net.
- Surplus size: novices **20–40% above maintenance**, advanced **10–20%**.
- Bulk:cut ratio for naturals **3:1 to 4:1**; bulks should run **≥6 months**.

## 10. Adherence, maintenance, weaning off tracking

- **"Adherence is everything."** A plan is only as good as how long it's followed. Only ~20% of dieters maintain loss; the goal is to move that number.
- Bodyweight is a **settling point**, not a fixed set point — shiftable by habit and environment.
- Build on the three psychological needs: **autonomy, competence, relatedness.** Individualize everything (macros, food choices, self-monitoring, meal frequency, non-linearity, cheat strategy).
- **Cheat meals / hedonic deviations:** planned indulgences integrated from the outset can *help* goal achievement. The Rock's "cheat" meals are just scheduled carb-ups. Clients respond very differently to a free-day allowance — test and individualize.
- **Plateaus** (a month+ of no change) have only two causes: broken adherence, or a new energy-balance equilibrium. Reframe them as **"maintenance practice."**
- **NEAT** is the most variable energy-expenditure component; the "metabolic slowdown" of dieting is mostly a drop in NEAT, not RMR. Hard-gainers ramp NEAT up under a surplus (a subject in one study burned 692 extra kcal/day of NEAT on a 1,000 kcal surplus).
- Shifting focus from scale weight to **performance improvement** can drive weight loss as a side effect — useful for burnt-out clients.
- Tracking is a double-edged sword: valuable early for awareness, but wean clients off constant micromanagement toward sustainable habits, watching for obsessiveness.
