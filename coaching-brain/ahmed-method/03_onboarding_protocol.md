# 03 — Onboarding Protocol (Data Collection)

> Exactly what Ahmed collects when a client signs on, why he collects each field, the follow-up questions, and the red flags he screens for. Grounded in the transcripts (onboarding calls: [Name], [Name], [Name], [Name] onboarding, [Name]; plus data-collection moments across all calls). An AI copilot onboarding a client should collect this dataset, in roughly this order, always explaining the "why," and always in a judgment-free tone.

---

## The onboarding flow (order of operations)

1. **Payment** (payment link on WhatsApp) — commitment comes before assessment.
2. **Create account** on the LFG coaching app → agree to terms/policy consent.
3. **Onboarding form** (auto-loads after payment) — detailed Q&A. "Please be very detailed. The more data, the better it is for me."
4. **Baseline capture:** body measurements AND/OR progress photos (at least one is mandatory), optional one-time body-fat scan.
5. **Buy the tools:** weighing scale, food scale, measuring tape (he sends Amazon links).
6. **Report gym equipment / access** (photos if they don't know equipment names).
7. He takes **~1 day to build the personalized program.**
8. Client is added to a **WhatsApp group** (client + Ahmed + Coach Omar) and the private client community group.

**Sequencing rule (stated):** "We onboard you first. We take the measurements, we get the commitment and the investment. Based on that, we do assessments." Money/commitment precedes the free physical assessment.

**The onboarding-call purpose** (stated): walk them through the app so they're not overwhelmed, review the workout program, review the nutrition document, and confirm all data. "There's so many things on the app — I don't want to overwhelm you."

## A. Anthropometrics & body composition

| Field | How he collects it | Why (his stated reason) |
|---|---|---|
| **Height, weight, age** | From intake form, confirmed live. | Baseline for calorie/macro calc and program level. |
| **Weight range / fluctuation** | Asks their typical low/high. | Understands their swing; sets realistic targets. |
| **Body-fat %** | ONE baseline scan allowed (bioimpedance smart scale, InBody, or Dr.Nutrition). He standardizes clients to a single device ("stick to one machine, all my clients use this"). | "Just for now, so I know if the calories I put for you are accurate. I can adjust later." |
| **Progress photos** | Front/side/back. Consent-based; offers face-cover + no-name; doubles as marketing asset. | Visual recomposition proof. "If you're okay, it helps my marketing — I cover the face, just before and after." |
| **Body measurements (tape)** | Client self-measures and reports numbers. Default for hijabi/conservative clients (no photos). | Physique tracking without the scale. |

**Explicit stance against over-relying on scans:** "The DEXA scan — first, maybe you pay money, and it's inconvenient. The free doctor-nutrition or gym smart scale isn't 100% accurate — there's a 3 to 8% inaccuracy in body-fat composition. So I don't depend only on it. I use all the factors: total weight (accurate), pictures or tape, and performance. Don't hyper-fixate on the body scan — we look at the **trend**."

**Self-measurement protocol he dictates** (so numbers are repeatable):
- **Bicep:** 4 fingers up from the elbow, at the index finger.
- **Thigh:** 8 fingers above the knee.
- **Hips/glutes:** widest part, wrapped tight.
- Also: chest, waist/stomach.
- "Take them the same way every week."

**The recomposition proof he teaches** (why he needs all three metrics): "If you're increasing your weight in the gym, decreasing your weight on the scale, and your measurements look different — your clothes fit different — that means you're building muscle and losing fat." Folk illustration: "One kilo of fat looks big; one kilo of muscle is small."

## B. The three weekly-tracked pillars

He tells every client the same three variables are what he watches each week:
1. **Body weight** (tracked daily to read fluctuation; viewed at a weekly-average/macro level, not micro — he shows clients a "sawtooth" weight graph to normalize spikes).
2. **Physique** (photos or tape measurements).
3. **Gym performance** (progressive overload — weights/reps/sets logged).

> "You dropped 0.5 kilos this week, point-something in your waist, and increased two kilos on this bench press. That's three stats."

## C. Wearables

- **Apple Watch** — for steps + heart-rate zones + workout-type logging. "You have a watch, right? Put your phone/watch on the treadmill so you see your heart rate."
- **Whoop** — connected for recovery/HR ("we're going to wear the whoop from now on"; "you can connect that").
- **Garmin** — uses its race-time predictions as running baseline data ("the Garmin is predicting 24 minutes for 5K").
- He teaches HR-zone use live (see file 04 for the 220−age math and talk-test).

---

## D. Daily habit-tracker inputs (logged in the LFG app, ~2 min)

Weight, steps/movement, sleep hours, sleep quality, stress, mood/journal, energy levels, soreness, water/hydration, meals, small wins, and **cycle** (female). Custom trackables can be added on request (e.g., cigarettes if quitting smoking, a pill, water target).

**Why (stated):** "When I check your performance in the gym and you went down in the weights — why? Then I go to the daily habits. Oh, you slept four hours, or you're traveling, or you're on your period. So I need to know this information." Data explains bad sessions.

**Note:** he'll de-emphasize daily food logging when eating is stable ("if you tell me you eat this five days a week, I already understand"), but keeps **food photos** as an accountability tool (see E).

---

## E. Nutrition intake

Collected in the onboarding form + a live 7-day food-photo diary in the WhatsApp group.

- **Meals per day, meal timing, and what the meals actually are** ("Is it a burger? Rice and chicken?"). He restructures 1–2-meal + night-snacking patterns.
- **Cook vs. order mix; lives alone vs. with family** ("when you're with family, it's hard in terms of cooking and food").
- **Food preferences, dislikes, allergies** ("Is there any food that you don't like?" / "Tell me about your allergies"). Notes cultural cuisine (Moroccan, Iraqi, Iranian, Indian, etc.).
- **Go-to restaurants / Talabat–Deliveroo history / cuisines** ("tell me what restaurants you typically order from, or just the cuisine — Arabic, Lebanese, Japanese").
- **Snacking:** frequency + sweet vs. savory ("Are you snacking with sweets or salty stuff?").
- **Protein sources + protein shake** — he audits the label live (ingredients, protein per scoop, calories per serving) and swaps to a no-sugar isolate if needed.
- **Caffeine/coffee** habits and reactions; **alcohol** ("Do you drink alcohol?").
- **Food photos going forward:** "Take a picture of your plate and send it. This is more psychological — I'm more accountable if I have to show it. You cannot cheat like this."

**Why food photos over typing (stated):** "It's easier than typing, gives us a clear idea, and makes you more aware of your plate. It's a judgment-free zone — we're not judging, just understanding so we can make it sustainable."

## F. Sleep, stress & lifestyle

- **Sleep** — bedtime, wake time, hours, and **quality** (distinguishes hours from deep/REM: "Many people sleep 8–9 hours but zero quality"). Probes what keeps them up ("On a regular basis, what keeps you up until 1, 2, 3 a.m.? Are you an overthinker?").
- **Steps** — weekly average from the watch; the number he raises gradually (never 5k→10k in a week).
- **Stress / mood / energy / soreness** — daily.
- **Schedule** — work hours, shift pattern, WFH vs. office, commute, weekend work.
- **Other activities** — Pilates, spinning, cycling, calisthenics, swimming, boxing/Muay Thai (flags high-intensity so he can predict soreness affecting his sessions: "let me know if you're doing something crazy like Muay Thai, because you'll be sore and might not perform on our program day").
- **Non-negotiables in the calendar** — "Do you have anything in your schedule that is non-negotiable? Like every day at 8am I walk my dog, every Friday the mosque." He builds the program around these. "The calendar is your best friend... this reduces the risk of inconsistency."

## G. Equipment & gym access

- Asks for **photos of the gym/home setup** ("list it or take pictures if you don't know the name").
- Inventories machines, dumbbells, bands, and reads their machine photos to adjust exercise instructions (e.g., adjustable-angle chest press; leg-extension vs. leg-curl).
- **Access type:** open gym vs. class-only, day-pass availability, track access days, treadmill brand (to translate pace into machine numbers), terrain (flat vs. elevation).
- **HYROX station-by-station audit** (for race clients): sled push, sled pull + rope, ski erg, row, sandbags, kettlebells, wall ball + wall target, medicine balls. Substitution logic on the spot: "No sandbags? Use dumbbells. Ski only needs training once a week, so a single day-pass visit covers it."

## H. Medical / red-flag screening (the safety layer)

He screens every client, women especially. Verbatim probes:

- **Injuries:** "Do you have any injuries — your knee — or have you ever had?" Probes root cause ("Is it the mobility in the lunges or touching the knee on the floor? You feel pain when you hit the floor?"). Cases seen: back injury/disc bulge, knee (scooter accident), asthma, neck disc bulge with arm numbness. Response: **Phase-1 light/movement-only, plus prehab** ("preventive maintenance") drawn from his own physio history: "I've had injuries in my hips and knees, went to a lot of physiotherapy, and I applied what works."
- **PCOS / insulin resistance** (asked of nearly every female client): "A lot of girls who train have PCOS — do you have that?" Links it to stress/cortisol: "It's insulin resistance, directly correlated to stress."
- **IBS / bloating** — named and worked around (a central presenting issue for some clients).
- **Menstrual cycle** — "Is it regular? Do you track it?" Framed medically: "This is normal with all my female clients. If we don't know this, we'll misunderstand your progress. It's as if you're talking to your doctor." Drives the Cycle Awareness Guide (files 04/05).
- **Postpartum** — confirms cleared, asks "are you still breastfeeding?" before programming.
- **Eating-disorder history** — if present (e.g., past bulimia), he deliberately avoids strict calorie restriction: "I don't want it so strict that you hate your food. As long as it's not too restrictive."
- **Heart condition / caffeine sensitivity / palpitations** — logged and worked around.
- **Medications** — actively discussed. He challenges quick fixes (Mounjaro/Ozempic, gastric surgery) and prefers building the base; cites a client he took off Mounjaro who then dropped ~9–10kg on basics. He raises contraceptive-pill concerns with some clients. **[inferred + caution]** This is his personal stance in the transcripts; an AI copilot should NOT advise stopping any prescribed medication — surface it, encourage the client to consult their doctor, and adapt training/nutrition around whatever the doctor decides.
- **Blood work / deficiencies** — recommends a general check but not required for Phase 1: "Check your blood work for deficiencies, then we can make a supplementation plan — but that's not your first phase."
- **Vaping / smoking / alcohol** — asked directly; can be tracked as a habit-goal in the app.

**Red flags in the *behavior* (not medical) he actively surfaces:**
- A stated goal untouched for a year ("It's been a year you've been trying").
- Start-stop history / the "excited-then-quits-in-three-weeks" pattern.
- Wanting to "start properly later" instead of now.
- All-or-nothing / scale-fixation mindset.

## I. The onboarding-call coaching questionnaire (communication setup)

Beyond the physical data, he asks how to *work with* the person:
- **Feedback preference:** video vs. text bullet points.
- **Top three personal values:** "These are super important — your pillars for self-belief — so I know how to communicate with you."
- **Motivation style:** "fast aggressive results" vs. "sustainable plan."
- **Willingness to embrace discomfort.**
- **Travel dates** put on a shared calendar.
- **Permission to push:** he confirms how hard the client wants to be pushed (one client: "please shout at me, I need that pressure" — he calibrates accordingly, while noting any injury he must protect).

---

## J. The tools he has clients buy (with links)

- **Weighing scale** (home) — daily weigh-in after the bathroom, before eating.
- **Food scale** (~AED 25 / ~$25 on Amazon) — to measure protein/portions. "When you do it frequently, in three months you'll eyeball it."
- **Measuring tape** — for weekly measurements.
- (Optional) a cheap Amazon smart scale for body-fat *trend*.

---

## K. Copilot onboarding checklist

1. Confirm payment/commitment, then create app account + terms.
2. Collect the full onboarding form (detailed).
3. Capture baseline: photos and/or tape (mandatory at least one) + optional one-time body-fat scan for the trend.
4. Have them buy weighing scale, food scale, measuring tape.
5. Get gym equipment/access (photos) — run the HYROX station audit if race-bound.
6. Screen medical red flags (injuries, PCOS/insulin resistance, IBS, cycle, postpartum, ED history, meds — never advise stopping prescribed meds; defer to their doctor).
7. Map the schedule + non-negotiables into a calendar.
8. Ask the communication questionnaire (feedback style, top-3 values, motivation style, push level, travel dates).
9. Explain the app: daily check-in, weekly check-in, workout logger ("don't forget to press complete"), food photos.
10. Set expectations warmly and set the first session/start date. Add to WhatsApp group + community group.
