# 05 — Nutrition Approach

## 1. Philosophy in one line

Nutrition is the biggest physique lever, and it must be **sustainable, personalized, and adherence-first** — never a strict, restrictive, Instagram diet.

- "If you want a good physique, it starts with nutrition — more than the workout itself. You can smash the gym, but if your nutrition isn't on point, there's really no point." (endorses ~70% framing: "When they say 70%, it's really 70%")
- "I don't want it so strict where you hate your food and eating feels like a chore. I actually want you to look forward to eating."
- "Nutrition is the biggest domino." (when asked what one thing to fix first, nutrition is usually his answer)

---

## 2. The 3M Framework (his flagship nutrition system)

He gives the client **three ways to control nutrition** so there's "zero excuse":

### M1 — Meals
- A meal-plan document: **3 options each** for breakfast/lunch/dinner, portion-controlled, macro-calculated, built to the client's preferences/allergies/cuisine/goal.
- "In an ideal scenario, you cook these and eat them every day and don't worry about macros or calories — you know they're within your target. No calculation."
- Rotate options to prevent boredom: "They'll do it for four weeks, then feel bored, so I give options." Nine core options "can stay for six months if you rotate them."
- **Meal-prep coaching:** batch-cook on WFH days, marinate, Tupperware, "book it in your calendar like a meeting." Rotate spices/proteins so "it's the same food but a different dish."

### M2 — Macros
- Teach protein/carbs/fat + calorie targets and label-reading, based on **BMR/maintenance (TDEE)**, so "you'll never not know how to gain, maintain, or lose weight." The fallback for eating outside the meal plan or using a meal-prep company.
- **Diet reframed:** "Diet doesn't mean losing weight — it's what macros you're eating."

### M3 — Market
- Grab-and-go lists from specific stores near the client (Spinneys, Waitrose, Carrefour, Lulu, ADNOC), using deli counters ("100g rice, 200g chicken"), plus how to order out and at gatherings/buffets.
- "So now you have three different ways to control your nutrition. Zero excuse."

## 3. Study first, don't prescribe first (sequencing)

**Habits before macros.** He deliberately delays the formal diet:
- "There's no point giving you macros — you'll be motivated two weeks, then after three weeks say 'I can't do it.' First I make sure you enjoy the habits and control portion sizes."
- "We study your food in the first week, see what you're having, and from there integrate more protein / decrease this / include that. A seamless transition into a more controlled diet."

**Beginner sequence = swap, don't diet.** For someone eating one junk meal a day, he refuses to start a diet:
- Phase-1 **simple swaps:** cake/biscuit → fruit or Greek yogurt + fruit; chips → popcorn or protein chips; milk chocolate → dark chocolate or dates; add vegetables/fiber.
- "You're just substituting something heavy in sugar for something less sugar and more healthy."
- Portion control over restriction: "You're eating the food you love — now instead of five chicken breasts, three. Instead of this much rice, a little less. Same food, controlled portion."

## 4. Setting targets

- Calculates **BMR → maintenance calories**, then a **slight deficit** (not aggressive). "I don't believe in a very harsh, aggressive cut. I look at your body composition and maintenance first, then a slight deficit."
- Example real prescription: ~1,700 kcal average, split ~120g protein / ~190–200g carbs / ~52g fat for a fat-loss/recomp female client — framed as "a decent cut, not aggressive, so it won't make you relapse if you eat outside."
- **Protein is the near-universal first target**, ~**30g per meal**, and the fix for snacking/under-eating: "Make sure every meal — breakfast, lunch, dinner — has protein." "The snacking isn't hunger, it's that you're not nutritionally fulfilled — you need enough protein."
- **Carbs are not the enemy:** "You're not cutting carbs. I'm not a believer in keto/zero-carbs." ~170–180g carbs/day for an active client (~60g/meal). "Carbs are energy — don't be afraid of carbs when training." Post-workout meal gets the day's highest carbs (insulin-sensitivity window).
- **Fat control:** "Minimize oil, reduce fried food. Air-fry, olive-oil spray, grill — not fry." Watch calorie-dense add-ins (nut milks, nut butters with added oils).

## 5. Adherence over perfection (the sustainability doctrine)

- **80/20:** "80% strict, 20% you enjoy." "You can still have the chocolate, the cake — but in control."
- **The 5-of-8 rule (his KPI):** on a daily checklist (protein each meal, 3 meals, water, steps, sugar protocol, etc.), "instead of eight out of eight, do at least five out of eight consistently. That's already a success. Five out of eight consistently beats eight one week and zero the next."
- Anti-restriction, especially for eating-disorder history: deliberately non-restrictive, builds cravings in rather than banning ("as long as it's not too restrictive, you're fine").
- **Calorie density / volume eating:** "1,000 calories of nuts vs. 1,000 calories of rice, chicken, salad — you'll still be hungry with the nuts because the volume is small. Fill up with more fiber, more volume, fewer calories. That's the trick."
- **Food sequencing (blood-sugar):** "Vegetables/fiber first, then protein, then carbs" to avoid glucose spikes. Frames it via restaurant "starters."

## 6. Preferences, allergies, culture, IBS, cycle

- **Preferences/allergies:** collected up front ("Is there any food you don't like? Tell me about your allergies") and honored in the meal plan (e.g., builds a plan around a client's oatmeal aversion, dairy-free needs, or a Moroccan/Iraqi cuisine).
- **Culture:** adjusts for cultural diets ("Middle Eastern people like to eat big protein — that works in our favor"; "I understand Asian families have a strong mentality") and family cooking constraints.
- **IBS / bloating:** worked around directly (e.g., isolating caffeine as the bloating trigger through testing, then advising eat-first-then-coffee). "Really good that we tested and tried different ways — at the end it was the caffeine."
- **PCOS / insulin resistance:** linked to stress management; nutrition and training adjusted accordingly.
- **Dairy/lactose:** swaps to oat/almond milk (watch calories), lactose-friendly protein.
- **Cycle nutrition (luteal/menstrual):** "Increase carbs a bit (rice 150→200g), increase greens, reduce salt/oil, and drop gym intensity to ~70%." Omega-3/salmon "to help manage your menstrual cycle"; magnesium for soreness/sleep.

## 7. Eating out, buffets, and travel

- **Plate method (rule of thumb):** half the plate protein, a quarter vegetables/fiber, a quarter carbs (rice/potato/sweet potato/bread; avoid pasta). Round two: wait 10 minutes, then **protein + veg only, no carbs.** Avoid cream/oil/sauce ("liquid calories").
- **Restaurant cuisine ranking (best → worst):** Arabic (rice + veg + big protein) → Mediterranean/Levant (grills, shish tawook, hummus) → European (ask for sauce on the side) → Italian (avoid: pasta, cream, low protein) → Asian (highest oil/fat: noodles, hot pot, Korean BBQ — avoid).
- **How to read a menu:** "Does it have protein? Too much sauce? Any vegetables/fiber? What's the carb — if it's pasta, find rice or roasted potatoes instead."
- **Buffet / conference survival hack:** eat a protein snack ~1 hour before, scan the whole spread first, then build the plate by the plate method, push the bread away, and always carry a protein/kind bar as backup.
- **Judgment-free food photos** going forward for accountability: "Take a picture of your plate — it's psychological, you can't cheat, and it makes you more aware."

## 8. Cravings & coffee protocols

**Sugar-craving protocol (in order):**
1. Drink ~500ml water and wait/walk/call a friend ("sometimes it's not craving, it's dehydration").
2. Still craving → a protein snack.
3. Last resort → dark chocolate, 1–2 squares (lint/85%+).

**Coffee/syrup strategy (the math):** "If you calculate the syrup, it's 300–500 calories per drink. Over a week that's ~2,000 calories just from coffee." Fix: syrupy coffee once a week; Americano/flat white otherwise, or one pump of syrup. "This makes a huge difference in bloating, cravings, and energy in week one."

**Caffeine sensitivity:** if it causes palpitations/bloating, minimize or microdose, or move to decaf; suggest **warm water + Himalayan salt** in the morning as a natural electrolyte ritual replacing coffee.

## 9. Snacking

- Snack = something small, "maximum ~200 calories," and **high protein.**
- Go-to snacks he prescribes: rice cake + tuna (or almond/peanut butter), Greek yogurt + berries, boiled egg, cucumber + hummus, mixed nuts, fruit, a protein shake.
- **Cold-pressed nut butter hack:** "At Carrefour's nuts section you can press peanuts/almonds/cashews into pure butter — no sugar, no added oil, and cheaper than jarred."
- If protein is adequate, snacking drops on its own: "If you have enough protein in your system, you won't snack much because you'll be full."

## 10. Supplements & scans

- **Protein powder:** audits the label (ingredients, protein/scoop, calories/serving); swaps to a no-added-sugar isolate. Vanilla to add to yogurt bowls.
- **Electrolytes:** flags redundant sodium stacks; recommends Himalayan salt in warm water as a cheaper natural alternative — "these brands are ripping everyone off."
- **Creatine/probiotics/etc.:** reviewed case-by-case; supplementation plan only after blood work, and "not in your first phase."
- **Body-fat scans:** one baseline is fine to set calories; then track the **trend**, not the absolute (see file 03).

## 11. Meal-prep companies & the fallback

- **Anti-meal-prep-company by default:** "I don't fully trust meal-prep companies — their macros aren't accurate and it's overpriced. Do it only if you truly can't cook (live alone, no time)."
- **Worst-case fallback:** if a client won't cook, give calories + macros (M2) and let them use a delivery company (e.g., Right Bite / Lifter Life / Practical) — "but the best results come from cooking at home."

## 12. Hydration, movement & the morning ritual

- **Water:** aims ~2–2.5L/day; starts lower if they resist ("every girl doesn't like drinking water — let's start at 2L"). Sparkling water instead of soda ("even Pepsi Zero is a mental thing that makes you crave sweet").
- **Morning ritual:** warm water + a pinch of Himalayan salt on waking — "natural electrolytes, opens the stomach and nervous system, keeps you hydrated all day." Positioned as a replacement for the morning coffee.
- **Steps as part of nutrition/energy balance:** raised gradually (see file 04), with the walk-and-talk / stairs / park-far hacks.

## 13. Race fueling (for endurance clients)

- Treated as a distinct skill in the peak block: find the right **gel** (brand/flavor/carb dose the stomach tolerates), time it, and test it in training — never on race day untested.
- **Carb-loading:** "Carbs are energy — don't be afraid of carbs when running high mileage."
- **Ramadan-specific:** break fast with 2–3 dates (not 6 — "high in sugar"), a soup if available, plenty of water, a grilled-protein + salad + steamed-rice iftar, and don't overeat from hunger. Suhoor prep ideas sent in advance ("easy, 10 minutes to prep").

## 14. Copilot nutrition checklist

1. Study current food first (photos, 7-day diary) before prescribing.
2. Beginners: swaps + portion control, not a diet. Delay macros until habits hold.
3. Set targets from BMR/maintenance with a slight deficit; protein-first (~30g/meal); don't fear carbs; control fat/oil.
4. Deliver the 3M: Meals (3 options each, portion-controlled), Macros (targets + label literacy), Market (store grab-and-go + eating out).
5. Honor preferences/allergies/culture; adjust for IBS/PCOS/cycle; never over-restrict (especially ED history).
6. Teach the plate method, cuisine ranking, buffet hack, sugar-craving protocol, coffee strategy.
7. Keep it 80/20; track the 5-of-8 daily wins; use judgment-free food photos for accountability.
8. Hydration + Himalayan-salt morning ritual + gradual steps.
9. For races: test fueling in training, carb-load smart, adapt for Ramadan.
10. Prefer home cooking; meal-prep company only as a can't-cook fallback (give macros).
