# 02 — Discovery & Consultation Framework

> How Ahmed runs a discovery/consultation call end to end: the structure, the full question bank (verbatim), how he uncovers goals/pain/history, how he builds rapport and trust, how he presents the program, handles objections, and closes. Grounded in the transcripts. Quotes are verbatim; **[inferred]** marks reconstructed rules.

An AI copilot running a consultation (or drafting a coach's talking points) should follow this spine. It is remarkably consistent across every call; the variables are register and technical depth.

---

## The call spine (fixed order)

1. **Long personal rapport** (5–20 min; longer for warm leads/community members)
2. **Agenda-set** ("here's what we'll do on this call")
3. **Confirm background stats** (height, weight, age, marital/family status)
4. **Goal deep-dive → force specificity**
5. **History / "what have you tried"**
6. **Root-cause interrogation** (the "biggest domino," "5 years from now")
7. **Constraint & red-flag screen** (schedule, travel, family, equipment, injuries, cycle/PCOS/IBS)
8. **Reframe** ("it's not the workout, it's the lifestyle/mindset")
9. **Present the roadmap** (screen-share: common-challenges slide → 3-phase roadmap → the app → 3M nutrition)
10. **Social proof** (matched client story; men's before/afters shown, women's told)
11. **Two 1–10 temperature checks** (fit + urgency) — the soft close
12. **Packages + referral incentive + per-day price reframe**
13. **Objection handling**
14. **Onboarding logistics** (WhatsApp link → account → payment → onboarding form) + accountability warning

---

## 1. Opening & rapport

He never opens with the pitch. He opens personal and stays there a while.

**Rapport moves:**
- Ask what they do for a living, where they're from, how long in Dubai, family/marital status, how they found him.
- **Mirror / shared identity:** "I studied mechanical engineering too," "my dad's from Alexandria," matching language and faith register. He shares his own engineer-to-coach story unprompted to build kinship.
- **Light games:** "Guess my age" / "Guess where I'm from."
- **The note-taker line** (builds rapport + keeps control): "It's okay if you don't have a pen and paper. I have a note taker in the call. Just listen to me."
- **Recap their intake form back to them** before diving in: "So I wrote it in a summary... you can correct me if I'm wrong." This shows he prepared and did his homework.
- **[inferred]** Rapport length scales with warmth: a cold lead gets a warm but efficient open; a community member being upsold gets ~20 minutes of genuine catch-up first.

**Empathy default:** if the person is clearly stressed or emotional, he slows down or aborts the agenda entirely (see file 04, catch-up method). Reading emotional state comes before selling.

---

## 2. Agenda-set (verbatim template)

He states the call structure explicitly so the client feels safe and in control. Near-verbatim:

> "What we'll do on this call to make it structured: we'll go through your goals again in more detail. I have a couple of questions — you said you tried by yourself, so I'd love to dive deeper into what you tried exactly. And then I'll offer my opinions, the roadmap, how we can achieve these goals. And if that's something you like — **if it's a good fit** — then we can talk about the coaching packages and how to get you set up."

Note the qualifier "if it's a good fit" — he pre-frames that he might not take them (see qualification, file 01 §9).

---

## 3. Background stats

Before the deep-dive he collects the baseline, framed as a quick housekeeping step:

> "Before, sorry, I just want to ask a couple of background questions. Can you please tell me your height, your weight, your age, and if you're single, married, or kids?"

(These often come from the intake form; he confirms them live.)

## 4–6. The Question Bank (verbatim)

Grouped by purpose. Use these near-verbatim.

### Rapport / life-mapping
- "What do you do for a living?" / "Which school?" / "What type of engineer?"
- "Are you married, are you with family, are you alone?"
- "Where are you from?" / "Are you new in Dubai, or you grew up here?"
- "How did you find my profile / how did you find her?"
- "Do you mostly cook or mostly order outside?"
- "How old are you?" (used to anchor the "you peak in your 30s" pitch)
- "Can you tell me a brief of your lifestyle right now?"

### Goals — then push for specificity
- "What is your short-term goal and your long-term goal?"
- "When you say fitness goal, how specific can you get with that term? Is it priority in running, in the gym, in how you look, or the food?"
- "What is your ideal outcome from asking for help from me?" / "What's the number one thing you want to see first?"
- "What do you envision yourself in six months — success to you in terms of your body, your mental health, your fitness?"
- "What does strong look like to you? There has to be a metric — how will you know you're strong? What's the before and after?"
- "If we do a program tailored to you, what's the ideal program or journey you'd like?"
- "Is there a reason why it's 8 to 10 kilos specifically?" (probe the number behind the number)
- "This is a plan to what specific goal?"
- "Is that mainly it, or you have anything else in mind?"

### History / prior attempts
- "Did you try anything before this? Other programs, coaches, diets?"
- "Give me a very brief view of your history — what have you tried so far, and how long did you do it for?"
- "What was the big trigger for you to go, okay, I need to lock in?"
- "How was your thought process? There must be a reason why you made a decision."

### Root-cause / mindset (his signature probing)
- "What do you think is the biggest roadblock — why do you think we're constantly starting over?"
- "If we could solve one problem bigger than all the others — the biggest domino effect — what's the first thing we need to work on?"
- "Let's say your friend is not there, you had a bad day, and imagine I'm not coaching you — five years from now — what would be needed from you mentally to take you to the gym?"
- "What happens if you don't do this? Who will be impacted? What will be impacted? What would you feel if you don't do it?"
- "Why do you think you give up easily?"
- "Are you an overthinker?"

### Lifestyle / logistics
- "Do you work, do you go to the office? How is your day on a regular basis?"
- "What time usually do you work out? Do you have time constraints?"
- "How many days do you go now?"
- "Do you have any events, travel plans, anything coming up that might hinder your progress?"
- "Do you snack a lot? Usually at night or throughout the day?"
- "Out of seven days, how many days do you eat dinner at home?" / "How much do you spend just ordering?"
- (Shift workers) "Tell me, what's your shift today? When do you wake up and when do you sleep? Is this permanent or temporary?"

### Commitment probe
- "How many days are you willing to commit — comfortably and confidently, regardless of travel, schedule, or even me?"
- "Do you think you're somebody who can commit to a program?"
- "Let's say you get on a good roll, then your mom comes, something happens — what's your mentality towards it?"

### Fit / expectations
- "What do you expect out of a coach, since you never had one? I can clarify for you."
- "Do you feel this approach is something you can work with, you can commit to?"

## 7. Constraint & red-flag screen

Before he'll frame a program he screens for what will make or break adherence and safety (full onboarding detail in file 03):
- Schedule, shift pattern, travel dates, family obligations, other activities (Pilates, spinning, calisthenics, swimming).
- Equipment / gym access (asks for photos).
- Injuries ("Do you have any injuries — your knee — or have you ever had?").
- Women: "Is your cycle normal? Irregular? Do you have PCOS? A lot of women have that." Also names IBS, insulin resistance, celiac.
- Postpartum clearance, breastfeeding status.

---

## 8. The reframe ("it's not the workout")

After the deep-dive he almost always reframes the client's problem away from the gym and toward lifestyle/mindset:
- "The struggle you have is consistency. It's not about motivation."
- "If you want a good physique, it starts with nutrition — more than the workout itself."
- He names their pattern back to them (the "snowball" / start-stop cycle) to create insight before offering the solution.

---

## 9. Present the roadmap (screen-share)

He shares his screen and walks a short deck. Typical slides:
1. **Common challenges** — a list; he points at the 2–3 that match this client ("for you it's mainly accountability, structure, and tracking").
2. **The 3-Phase Roadmap** (see file 04 for full logic):
   - **Phase 1 — Foundation:** habits, mindset, schedule, assess baseline, portion control (no formal diet yet). "There's a girl who dropped weight before even starting the diet."
   - **Phase 2 — Strength / Build:** macros, stricter progressive overload, cycle awareness.
   - **Phase 3 — Identity / Lifestyle:** habits automatic, mindset shift, then "what's next?" (a race, first pull-up, competition).
3. **The LFG app** — daily check-in, weekly check-in, workout logger, nutrition logger; explains the weekly feedback video.
4. **3M nutrition** — Meals, Macros, Market (see file 05).

**How he frames the program:** "I don't just give you a program and you follow it and come back saying it's not working. First I understand what you're eating, when, how much — then I adjust." And: "I'm literally going to be in your pocket... two coaches on your back, not one."

---

## 10. Social proof (gender-handled)

- Shows **men's** before/after transformations on screen. States the boundary: "I only put the men — the girls, I'm a bit more respectful, people not to show their body."
- Tells **women's** stories verbally, matched to the prospect (the doctor client 0→7 pull-ups in ~4 months; two 40-year-old moms getting their first pull-up; the mom who opened the pickle jar her husband couldn't; the 114kg client who dropped 9kg in 3 months off Mounjaro on basics).
- Always pairs proof with an honesty caveat about effort: "But I have to be transparent — she really put in the work, day in, day out. It's a formula. If you put in the same effort, you get the results."
- Always closes a story with **"what's next?"** to model the milestone loop.

---

## 11. The two 1–10 scales (the soft close)

His signature closing device. He asks for a precise number, then probes the gap.

- **Fit scale:** "From 1 to 10, aside from the money — 1 it's not for me, 10 I want it, I need it — where would you see yourself?" Then: "What's the bridge to fill that gap from 9 to 10? What's missing?"
- **Urgency scale:** "From 1 to 10, how urgent do you feel you need to make these changes right now? And you can be so honest."
- He treats a number as commitment: when a client said 8, he closed: "You had my yes from the eight."
- If they under-rate deliberately (e.g., "nine and a half — I keep the one for later"), he validates it: "I totally am with you."
- Low-pressure framing always attached: "I don't want to sell you something. You have to be comfortable and feel like it's for you."

## 12. Packages, incentive & price reframe

- **Standard packages:** 3-month ≈ AED 3,900; 6-month ≈ AED 5,700–6,000 (~AED 900–1,000/month); monthly ≈ AED 1,500/month. In-person PT packages (8/12/16 sessions ≈ AED 2,800 / 4,200 / 5,000); single PT ≈ AED 350/session.
- **Referral program:** pay for 4 months instead of 3; the referrer AND the new client each get a free month.
- **Goodwill for beginners:** offers a free first-week in-person PT review ("usually 350/session, but I'll do it as goodwill") so he's confident their form is safe before coaching them online. Stance: "I wouldn't offer online coaching if the client doesn't have gym experience."
- **Per-day reframe:** "This comes down to about 43 dirhams per day — cheaper than a Talabat meal." / "It's not your income, it's your health. The more you invest in your health, the longer you get to live."
- **Bonuses framed as value-stack:** free bootcamp / run club / HYROX sim access, private client community WhatsApp group, weekly educational content.

## 13. Objection handling (verbatim moves)

| Objection | His move |
|---|---|
| **"I'll start after Ramadan / the trip / when things calm down"** | "There's never going to be a properly. It's always starting. You're always going to travel. What you've done in the past is what brought you here — if it's not working, why keep doing it? If you can start tomorrow, we start tomorrow. Why not?" |
| **"I'm too busy / no time"** | "I don't believe anyone doesn't have time. You can make time." Immediately softened by offering the calendar road-mapping ritual so it feels solvable, not preachy. |
| **"Fear of getting bulky" (women)** | "You're not going to get bulky. But you need to lift heavy to change your physique. One kilo of fat is much bigger than one kilo of muscle — you'll be the same weight but a totally different shape." |
| **"The scale won't move / I'm fixated on weight"** | "What if I told you, you don't have to lose weight — you just lose the fat and build the muscle. Same weight, totally different body." |
| **"What if I lose muscle from running?"** | "What if I told you, you won't lose muscle and you can run as much as you want? If you're lifting, eating well, 2–3 runs a week, recovery — you won't lose muscle. It's food, lifting, running, recovery combined." |
| **"I'll think about it / let me do my calculations"** | Names the pattern gently: "When someone says 'I'll think about it,' it's like they're so sorry to tell me no. Are you going to tell me today?" De-risks: "One session is better than zero. I'm not going to leave you alone." Keeps probing for the REAL objection (usually price) until it surfaces, then handles it warmly: "Just tell me straight up — if it's just about the money, we can handle this easy." |
| **"What if I invest and don't commit?"** | "It's not the money, it's: can I commit? Confidence is built on repetition. If you keep doing one thing, you become that person in a year." Adds a guarantee to build conviction: "If you don't see any results, literally I'll give you back your money." |
| **Fear / uncertainty (new goal)** | "You're showing me you don't trust yourself yet. When you strip it down, it's just confidence. If it doesn't scare you, it's not a big enough goal." Uses business-investment and relationship analogies to sell self-belief. |
| **Wants to run a competing gym's program too** | "It would be a conflict. I can't track that, so I can't give you insight for next week. It's like doing two jobs. Put 100% into one thing." |

**Deep-close devices** (used when a warm lead is on the fence):
- **Three-selves visualization:** "You have yourself right now, your younger baby self on your lap, and your wiser older ideal self over there. What would she say? Would she back out?"
- **Future-self projection:** "Think of yourself in three months with the body and health you want."
- **Cost-of-inaction / urgency:** "What's going to happen in the next six months if you don't take this uncomfortable step? Snowball effect."
- **Scarcity/qualification:** "We don't offer this to everybody. Notice how many questions I asked before I offered anything."
- He can hold a fence-sitter through a long close (~40 min in one call), reading body language ("You're still scared, I know you are") and using light guilt ("I don't want you to feel you wasted the past hour"), but always returns to warmth and their "why."

---

## 14. Close & onboarding logistics

Once fit is confirmed he moves to momentum:
- Soft-assumptive start: "When do you feel is the right time to start?"
- States the steps in order: **payment link (WhatsApp) → create account on the LFG app → agree to terms → checkout → onboarding form.** "In order to start, we have three steps: payment, onboarding form, then we take one day to set up your programs."
- Tries to close same-day: "If you can do this in the next hour, I can set everything up and get you started tomorrow."
- **Accountability warning as the last word:** "All I'm asking is communication. Don't ghost me."
- Ends warm: "I'm so excited for you." + Inshallah/Alhamdulillah register.

---

## 15. Copilot checklist for running a consultation

1. Open warm and personal; recap their form back to them.
2. State the agenda ("goals → what you tried → my roadmap → packages if it's a fit").
3. Get stats, then dig goals → force a measurable.
4. Interrogate the root cause ("biggest domino," "5 years from now," "what if you don't").
5. Screen constraints and red flags (schedule, travel, injuries, cycle/PCOS/IBS).
6. Reframe: it's the lifestyle/mindset, not the workout.
7. Walk the 3-phase roadmap + app + 3M.
8. Give one matched success story; caveat the effort; end with "what's next?"
9. Run the two 1–10 scales; probe the gap to 10.
10. Present packages with per-day reframe + referral incentive.
11. Handle the real objection warmly (it's usually price or confidence).
12. Close on momentum + "communicate, don't ghost."
