# 04 — Programming Approach (Training)

> How Ahmed designs and adapts training: his logic, heuristics, splits, progression, the 3-phase roadmap, running/HYROX/hybrid blending, adapting to injury/equipment/schedule, the weekly tracking loop, cycle-based training, and how he runs an ongoing check-in. ## 1. First principle: no templates — reverse-engineer from the goal, schedule, and equipment

- "There's no point for me to give you a workout plan that doesn't suit your goals and your body. You tell me 'I want to run a marathon,' I make a marathon program. HYROX, a HYROX program. I'm not going to give you a template."
- "We give you the program based on your schedule, your flexibility, your timings. Five days a week? I give five. Three? I give three. Tailored to your goals and your equipment. It's not a PDF."
- Real coaching = **data tracking + progression decisions**, not just showing form: "The real coaching is tracking your data, seeing where the loopholes are, where you're lacking, and structuring the workouts as you go."

**Assess baseline first, then place on a track (beginner vs. intermediate).** He can project outcomes from early logs: "If I know your logs, I can project roughly what time you'll do in the race."

---

## 2. The 3-Phase Roadmap (the backbone of every program)

Block-based, not fixed-day. A block runs **~4–6 weeks** for progressive overload, but is adjusted by *progress*, not the calendar ("if in three weeks you're stagnant, I change it — your muscles need to adapt again").

### Phase 1 — Foundation (~3–6 weeks; the most important phase)
- Goal: habits, mindset, schedule, movement, metabolism activation, portion control. Assess baseline. **No formal diet yet.** For beginners, front-load daily steps + simple movement before heavy lifting.
- "The goal of Phase 1 is to feel safe and change the misconception about working out."
- "The most important is Phase 1. If you do Phase 1 correctly, Phase 2 is smoother and Phase 3 is easy."
- Result often shows before the diet even starts: "There's a girl who dropped weight before even starting the diet."

### Phase 2 — Strength / Build (~6–8 weeks)
- Introduce structured strength, stricter **progressive overload**, and macros. Add cycle awareness for women.
- "This is where you build."

### Phase 3 — Identity / Growth (milestone-driven)
- Habits are automatic; mindset has shifted. Attach a milestone: a race (HYROX/marathon), a first pull-up, a dress size, a photoshoot. Then ask **"what's next?"**
- "This is now where you become who you wanted in your vision."

## 3. Split logic & structure

- **Start 2–3 days/week, scale to 4** as they improve. "Two times a week consistently every week beats five days for one week then nothing."
- Common splits: **upper / lower**, **lower+glutes / full-body / upper**, sometimes + a Pilates/recovery day.
- He assigns days flexibly: "I don't care if you train Monday–Thursday or Thursday–Sunday. As long as you finish it in the week and I can review it. It's like homework."
- For 2×2 shift workers: 3 sessions — two on off-days plus one on the last shift day "back to back."

---

## 4. Progressive overload — the non-negotiable engine

- "To build muscle and a good shape, you have to progressive overload — lifting heavier week by week." Tracked weekly (weights, reps, sets, tempo, grip).
- Weekly he checks: "Are you progressive overloading or not? Decreasing, increasing, or maintaining the weights?"
- **First week = a testing/form week.** "You're testing everything — what weight is good for me. Pick a weight where the top of the rep range is your true max. If you can do more than 12, it's too light, increase it. Week one: quality and form. Week two onward: smash the weights."
- Effort standard for women (against the 60–70% habit): "You need to lift heavy to change your physique. If you feel 'oh that's enough' at 60–70%, it's not enough. Do it till the muscle is begging to stop."

---

## 5. Strength over HIIT (a strong, repeated stance)

- "I need you to do lifting, not HIIT. HIIT isn't measurable. When you do lifting/resistance training, it's measurable — I know if your strength is increasing."
- Cardio/spinning/cycling is "good for sweating" and heart health but **cannot build muscle**; muscle drives fat loss and metabolism, so lifting is mandatory. He lets clients KEEP a loved cardio class alongside lifting, not instead of it: "If you want to lose 10 kilos of fat, you have to lift — two, max three times a week. You can keep your spinning, but the lifting is what changes your body."
- **Anti-variety:** "If you keep doing so many different exercises and different classes, your body is under a lot of stress but no progression. The fundamentals are boring. Rinse and repeat."

## 6. Adapting to injury, equipment, and schedule

**Injury** → regress, go light, add prehab:
- Default fix is a **regression** — an easier variation hitting the same muscle. He verifies form via **client-submitted video** over WhatsApp ("send me a video, I'll tell you what to tweak"), with a measurement-only, no-video option for conservative clients.
- Knee-hits-floor pain on lunges → "a lot of leg and lower-body endurance work — this also helps your wall balls."
- Back/neck/knee cases → a "no heavy lifting" Phase 1 + "preventive maintenance" prehab from his own physio experience.

**Equipment** → substitute:
- Dumbbells for sandbags; a single day-pass gym visit to cover the one machine they lack (e.g., ski erg, "the ski can be once a week").
- Reads the client's machine photos and adjusts instructions (adjustable-angle chest press; leg-extension not curl).
- No gym → bodyweight/calisthenics; travel → hotel-gym photo → custom program, or pack **resistance/booty bands** for upper+lower.

**Schedule** → road-map the calendar together:
- **Road-mapping ritual:** "We open your calendar and do road mapping — over here this run, over here this workout. We look at your non-negotiables, what to fix, and what's flexible." He fits training around family/friends/prayer, never over the top of them.
- Shift workers / heavy travelers: "Every week or every month, we sit down and re-align the schedule."

**Won't allow parallel external programs** (that he can't track): "If you replace my workouts with a ladies' class, I can't track it, so I can't give you next-week insight. It'd be a conflict — like doing two jobs. Put 100% into one thing."

## 7. One priority at a time (the Gantt-chart / periodization heuristic)

- "When you want to do everything, it's called a Gantt chart. You do periodization. Building a house, you don't start with the furniture. Choose the top three, do one correctly, move to the next."
- He borrows from his own team's training cycles ("HYROX season, then marathon season") to teach clients to sequence goals rather than chase all at once.

## 8. Running programming

**Baseline first:** "Do a 30-minute run and see your distance," measure best 5K/10K, use Garmin predictions.

**Periodized in phases** (most detailed in the half-marathon build):
1. **Foundation** — aerobic base, Zone 2, 2–3 runs/week, treadmill-first (in UAE summer, "outside your heart rate goes to Zone 5 from the heat").
2. **Build** — increase mileage; introduce **tempo** ("running fast and slow — more mileage than a single slow phase, and it makes you faster").
3. **Peak** — highest volume; **race simulations** ("join a 10K/15K — running in a race environment is completely different from training").
4. **Taper** — reduce volume ~3 weeks out; focus on fueling, pacing plan, shakeout runs; final week just a 5K shakeout.

**The four run types he programs:** easy runs, intervals, long runs, tempo runs. Standard = **3 runs/week**.

**How he prescribes intensity:**
- **Easy runs by heart rate, not fixed speed:** "If I give you a speed, maybe it's easy for me and hard for you. So I tell you a HR range and you adjust the treadmill." Zone 2 ≈ 130–145 BPM.
- **HR-zone math taught live:** max HR = 220 − age; Zone 2 = ~65–80% of max (~70% average). "For a 28-year-old: 220−28 = 192 max, ~70% ≈ 134. So 130–145 is Zone 2."
- **The talk-test as the field cue:** "Have a call with a friend while running. If you can talk — 'hey, how are you, I'm good' — you're controlled. If it's 'h-hi... how... are... you' — slow down."
- **Tempo by pace; intervals by RPE:** "200m at 8/10 — you can't speak. 600m at 5/10 — you can hold a conversation." 400m repeats, beat your prior time.

**Progression is gradual and injury-averse:** "If you can't run a 5K one shot, we start progressive overloading the easy runs. I do a test, then weekly I increase/decrease/adjust. We open the third run only when your body responds. We don't risk injury."

**Strength complements running, doesn't compete with it:** "Strong legs to endure two hours of running" — but he caps strength volume so soreness doesn't ruin runs ("if your goal is the race, running is the priority, strength complements it").

**Running caveat he warns about:** fast to gain, fast to lose — "if you stop, you start again from scratch."

## 9. HYROX programming (his most detailed specialty)

- **Running is ~70% of the race and the priority:** "70% of HYROX is running, so we prioritize that." Same four run types; if possible, **two track days (one intervals, one tempo).**
- **"Compromised running" is the core insight he teaches:** "Running a 5K fresh is different from running 1km in HYROX with fatigued legs. So we condition your legs to endure that intensity." He supplements weak stations (wall balls, burpees) with fatigued-leg runs.
- **Teaches the race format itself:** track → rock zone (40–70m) → station, stations in order (ski first), 8 stations, ~8.something km total.
- **Workout structure:** "one engine (conditioning/anaerobic), one power (strength)," plus a full-body day → **~4 workouts/week** alongside 3 runs. He splits distribution to the client's week ("two on the weekend, one after school — up to you, as long as it's done").
- **Lower-body/leg endurance emphasis** carries into sled push/pull and wall-ball stations.
- **EMOM-style conditioning** for class-format gyms ("45 min on, 20 sec off, 14 exercises alternating one strength/one cardio").
- **Race simulations** built in ~8 weeks out ("eight-week build to the race"), often Friday sims in person; if a client misses one, they do the equivalent session solo.
- **Race goal calibration:** average woman in her late 20s ≈ 1:30–1:50 solo; he projects a realistic time from early logs and frames a faster time as "a bonus, not pressure." For doubles-vs-solo, he usually recommends beginners start with doubles (less scary), but respects a client's mentality reason to go solo and just "beat your time next race."
- **Framing HYROX as a gateway:** "This opens a whole door — you'll start wanting more." Cites clients doing 2–3 HYROX events a year after one program cycle.

## 10. Blending modalities (running + strength + HYROX + swimming + calisthenics)

- He runs bootcamp (HIIT/circuit, social, once a week), a run club, strength camps, and HYROX classes, and folds them in as **free community/accountability bonuses** — but is clear they're not the training driver: "Bootcamp is more of a social get-together. That's why we do it once a week."
- Other client activities (Pilates, spinning, boxing) are accommodated but flagged for soreness that could affect his programmed sessions.
- **[inferred]** For a client blending, e.g., swimming + strength + a run goal, he'd apply the same logic: name the primary goal, make that the priority, let the others be complementary/enjoyment, and track only what he programs.

## 11. Women & "bulk" education (near-scripted, delivered to every female client)

- Fear of getting bulky → density explanation: "One kilo of fat is about double/triple the size of one kilo of muscle."
- **Body recomposition:** "Your body fat goes down, muscle goes up, but you're the same weight — a totally different shape." Same-weight-different-body illustration: "two women at 70kg — one looks overweight, one looks muscular."
- Redefines "tone": "You're losing fat and exposing the muscle. That's the actual word." "Bulk" is corrected to "calorie surplus."
- Metabolism framing: "More muscle burns more calories even while sleeping. Right now your metabolism is low; to raise it, more protein and more muscle."

## 12. Cycle-based training (the Cycle Awareness Guide)

Programs and effort around the four phases (structure stays the same; **effort** flexes):
- **Follicular** — push performance.
- **Ovulation** — peak; time PRs / heaviest lifts. "You're feeling 10 out of 10."
- **Luteal** — "reduce, don't stop. Instead of 10kg, lift 5. Instead of 10km, run 5. You still show up. Same structure, less effort."
- **Menstrual** — case-by-case; listen to the body; drop weights to ~70%; don't risk injury.
- **Why (stated):** "Many girls give up when they're not progressing in the luteal phase — their weight went up a kilo — 'I don't want to train anymore.' This needs patience. We track your phase so we don't misread your progress."

## 13. Steps & Zone-2 cardio (fat-burning + NEAT)

- Steps built **gradually**: "You can't go 5,000 to 10,000 in a week. Get 4,000 up to 6,000, make that the standard, then 8,000." Steps can be broken up (not continuous) with hacks: park far, take the stairs, walk-and-talk on calls, walking pad.
- Low-impact cardio prescribed as, e.g., **4 × 20-minute sessions/week** on any machine (treadmill/row/elliptical/bike), spread through the week, to raise NEAT and metabolism. Zone-2 HR + talk-test as above.

## 14. The weekly tracking & feedback loop (the coaching spine)

- **Daily check-in** (~2 min in the app): weight, steps, sleep, energy, stress, soreness, water, mood, cycle.
- **Workout logging:** weight/reps/sets/rest — "don't forget to press **complete**, or it won't reflect on my system." Verifies progressive overload week to week.
- **Weekly check-in form** (every Sunday): weight, physique (photo/tape), gym performance, plus wins/challenges/what-help-they-need.
- **Weekly feedback video** (recorded screen-recording, ~5–20 min, sent Monday on WhatsApp): reviews the whole week, compares week-over-week, assigns **1–3 focus points**. "I go through your whole week and adjust the program, rather than giving you a program and leaving you."
- Escalates to a call only for the most non-adherent clients.
- **The "1% every week" standard:** "Every week we need some type of win, at least 1%. We can't let you follow a program and not know whether you're progressing."
- **KPIs named for running clients:** "distance improving, feeling more energetic, progressive overload increasing, heart rate more stable in workouts."

## 15. Running an ongoing check-in / catch-up (retention & re-motivation)

From the catch-up calls:
1. **Read emotional state first — abort the agenda if needed.** When a client was crying: "I think this call is at a very wrong time. We can do it another time. Tell me whatever I can do to help. I'm here." Empathy before any coaching content.
2. **Structured review:** rate the month/period 1–10; revisit the original pain ("where were you before we started, what were you struggling with?"); name and **celebrate the wins** (bloating gone, weight down, first 5K, stopped night-binging).
3. **Capture a testimonial on the spot** — coach what to say, keep it natural ("I'll ask you and you answer, we'll make it more natural"), get before/after context.
4. **Answer real-time tactical questions** (fasted morning training, what to order for a work iftar, Suhoor prep).
5. **Nudge to log data** — "The more data we have, the better we can help you. Please log your workouts and daily habits."
6. **Diagnose inconsistency as a *structure* problem, not a discipline flaw:** "Inconsistency isn't a discipline problem, it's a structure problem. Your shift work plus Ramadan is one of the hardest environments to build consistency in."
7. **Re-sell the next block** by surfacing the real (usually financial) objection and handling it warmly, framing their results as his own portfolio: "If you give me a nice result, that's already my investment." Counters "let me try alone" with the teeth-brushing metaphor and "you could have done it already before."

## 16. Copilot programming checklist

1. Name the primary goal; make it the priority; other activities are complementary.
2. Assess baseline; place on beginner vs. intermediate; front-load Phase-1 foundation.
3. Build to the schedule and equipment; road-map the calendar around non-negotiables.
4. Program progressive overload as the engine; week one is a form/testing week.
5. Prefer measurable resistance training over HIIT; keep loved cardio as a complement, not a replacement.
6. For running/HYROX: baseline → periodize (foundation/build/peak/taper) → 3 runs (easy by HR, tempo by pace, intervals by RPE) + 3–4 strength/conditioning; teach compromised running and the race format.
7. Regress for injuries, substitute for equipment, verify form via video.
8. Flex effort (not structure) across the menstrual cycle.
9. Raise steps gradually; add Zone-2 cardio for NEAT/metabolism.
10. Run the weekly loop: daily check-in → workout logs → Sunday form → Monday feedback video with 1–3 focus points; target ≥1% weekly win.
11. On catch-ups: empathy first, celebrate wins, capture testimonial, nudge logging, diagnose structure over discipline, plant "what's next?"
